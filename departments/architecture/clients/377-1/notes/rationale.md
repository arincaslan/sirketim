## 2. Why the building is placed and oriented as it is

Unchanged from the single-level pass for the ground floor (long axis east-west, entry/circulation toward the street, living/kitchen toward the rear, footprint anchored to the front setback line). Extended this pass to the full vertical stack:

- **Vertical program**: bodrum (parking/service, below grade) → zemin (entry/social) → 1./2./3. normal kat (bedrooms, identical program each) → çatı piyesi (small terrace/mechanical enclosure). This is the standard stacking logic for a Turkish single-family residence of this scale, and puts wet zones (kitchen at zemin, bathrooms at each normal kat) in a consistent vertical column for plumbing-riser efficiency — unchanged reasoning from the single-level pass, now actually realized across all 4 above-grade levels instead of asserted for levels that weren't drawn.
- **Why each level isn't the same footprint size** (a real, new design decision this pass had to make, not present in the single-level case): the ground floor's program (108 m² net) needs a much bigger footprint than any single normal kat's bedroom program (50 m² net) — by design, per `brief.md`'s own concept narrative ("doesn't need anywhere near the full footprint on every floor"). Rather than force every level to share one footprint (which would either waste the bedroom floors' program inside an oversized envelope, or under-build the ground floor to match the bedrooms), each level here is sized independently from its own program, same philosophy the single-level generator already used for the ground floor alone. **This is a genuine simplification versus real reinforced-concrete construction**, where a building normally keeps one consistent structural footprint stacked floor-to-floor (walls carry load straight down); this schematic generator does not yet model that structural constraint, and a real structural engineer would very likely converge the upper floors' footprint back toward the ground floor's for load-path reasons. Flagged here plainly, not smoothed over — see §4.
- **Why the normal kat / çatı piyesi footprints are narrower, not full-width**: the ground floor and basement footprints use the existing full-envelope-width sizing (`compute_footprint()`), which spans the entire ~30m buildable width and varies only depth. Applied naively to the much smaller bedroom-floor program (50 m² net → ~58.8 m² gross footprint), that approach would produce a footprint only ~1.96m deep across the full 30m width — an unbuildable sliver, not a real floor plate (a bedroom drawn 10m wide × 2m deep is not a bedroom). This pass built a new function, `compute_footprint_fitted()`, for this — and along the way found (empirically, by actually inspecting the first generated sheet, not by assumption) that the first, naive version of that fix was still wrong: simply making the OVERALL footprint square (width ≈ depth ≈ √area, giving a ~7.67m × 7.67m footprint for a normal kat) still produces sliver rooms, just rotated 90° — because `build_level_geometry()`'s single-row bay-slicing always divides whatever footprint it's given into `len(program)` bays side by side, each spanning the FULL footprint depth. 5 bays across a 7.67m-wide footprint are still ~1.5m wide × 7.67m deep slivers. The function was corrected to size for square-ish INDIVIDUAL BAYS instead (`depth = √(area / n_bays)`, `width = n_bays × depth`) — producing a **~17.15m × 3.43m** footprint for each normal kat (58.82 m², 5 rooms) and a **~7.52m × 3.76m** footprint for the çatı piyesi (28.24 m², 2 rooms), both giving individual rooms roughly a plausible 1.5–1.7:1 aspect ratio rather than an extreme sliver in either direction (e.g. the 1st normal kat's master bedroom bay comes out ~5.7m × 3.43m ≈ 18.1 m² — a believable bedroom shape, not a corridor). Used for levels 1/2/3/4; the ground floor and basement keep the original full-width sizing since their larger, fewer-room programs already produce sensible bay proportions that way (the ground floor's 4 rooms across ~30m already come out ~7.4m × 4.0m per bay).

## 4. Tradeoffs and honest limitations (this pass)

- **Per-level independent footprint sizing, not one consistent structural footprint.** As explained in §2, each level (basement through roof piyes) is sized from its own program independently, rather than all above-grade levels sharing one stacked structural footprint the way a real reinforced-concrete building normally would. This is the single biggest new simplification this pass introduces, worth restating plainly: **a structural engineer designing this building for real would very likely need to reconcile the ground floor's larger footprint with the narrower bedroom floors above** (either by widening the upper floors, narrowing the ground floor, or introducing a genuine structural transfer — none of which this schematic tool models). `lib/cadgen` has no structural/load-path awareness at all; this is architectural-schematic-only output, consistent with the department's standing scope.
- **Basement/piyes KAKS-TAKS exclusion applied at the whole-level, not per-room.** The entire basement footprint is excluded from KAKS on the assumption its whole program is parking/service (plan notes item 4.2.1 actually exempts specific room types, e.g. parking and a small depo allowance, not necessarily every basement room such as the Çok Amaçlı Oda or Çamaşır Odası). A real permit submission would need to itemize this room-by-room; this pass's whole-level exclusion is a reportable simplification, not a literal application of the cited plan-notes item.
- **Single-row bay-slicing layout, still.** Unchanged limitation from the single-level pass — every level's rooms are laid out in one row of full-depth bays; this is why the ground floor consolidates into 4 zones rather than every itemized room, and why "Hol" and "Balkon" don't appear as separately-bounded spaces on the normal kat sheets (Balkon in particular is an exterior projection into the setback that this layout algorithm has no way to represent at all — excluded from concrete geometry entirely, same as it was implicitly excluded from the ground floor's 4-zone consolidation).
- **Basement and çatı piyesi room programs/areas are the department's own schematic estimates, not sourced from `brief.md`'s tables** (which didn't specify them) — see §1 for the exact numbers and why.
- **`zoning.max_height` re-derived from 14.50m to 14.65m this pass** (see §1) — a correction, and the compliance check now genuinely exercises the full 5-level height stack instead of trivially passing against a single drawn level, closing the exact gap the single-level pass's own rationale flagged ("Height check is only trivially exercised").
- **What did NOT get built this pass, despite being the stated target ("full production set" matching the founder's 373-6 reference DWG's depth):** `schedule.py` (door/window schedules), `elevation.py`, `section.py`, `finishes.py`, `furnishing.py` — **none of these exist yet**. This pass's scope was entirely the multi-level floor-plan generation + compliance-verification extension to `lib/cadgen/plan.py`; building 5 more substantial generator modules (each a real engineering effort — schedules need a cross-sheet mark-numbering scheme not yet designed, elevations/sections need a true 3D wall/opening model beyond this plan-view-only library, finishes/furnishing need their own data model extensions) is genuinely multi-session work, consistent with what the founder's brief for this pass explicitly anticipated ("budget for it being multi-session and say so honestly"). **Because none of these modules exist, the approval-stamp guard extension the founder's brief asked for ("extend the guard to schedule/elevation/section text too") has nothing new to protect yet** — no new text-writing code was added this pass beyond the info-line wording fixes in `plan.py`'s existing `render_dxf()`/`render_svg()`. **Correction (later audit pass, same initiative):** the claim above that this text was "fully covered by the existing `assert_no_stamp_language()` calls" was true for the DXF path (`render_dxf()`'s `note_text` was guarded) but **false for the SVG path** — `render_svg()`'s per-sheet `info_lines` block (the exact text rewritten this pass) was drawn via `dwg.add(dwg.text(line, ...))` with no guard call at all, a real live gap in text that had just been rewritten, not a dormant pre-existing one. This has now been fixed: `render_svg()` screens `"\n".join(info_lines)` through `assert_no_stamp_language()` before drawing any of the lines, matching the DXF path. `synthetic_lot_demo.py` re-run and still passes after this fix. Low-risk, not-yet-fixed secondary item flagged by the same audit: door/window mark labels (`"D1"`, etc.) and room name/area labels in `plan.py`'s `render_dxf()`/`render_svg()` are also unguarded — lower risk since they're short fixed-vocabulary strings, not free-form text, but worth guarding eventually. **Update (§7, schedule.py pass):** the newly-built `schedule.py` does NOT repeat this gap — every string it draws, including every table cell value (marks, widths, host wall IDs, etc.), is screened via `assert_no_stamp_language()` before being added to a drawing, verified in `synthetic_lot_demo.py`'s new §7d. The `plan.py` mark/room-label gap flagged above is unrelated and still open.
- **Layer convention**: adopted the 373-6 sample's cleaned AIA/NCS-style naming this pass (dropping the confirmed-dead `S-STRS` layer — which was never present in this library's own `LAYERS` dict to begin with, so nothing needed removing) and audited every layer this library actually declares (`plan.LAYERS`, 12 layers) for dead/unused entries across all 6 newly-generated sheets: **zero dead layers found** — every declared layer has at least one entity somewhere in the 6-sheet set (see the layer-usage audit output; `A-ANNO-DIMS` 48, `A-ANNO-TTLB` 84, `A-WALL` 24, `A-GLAZ` 63, etc., none at 0). This library's layer set is much smaller than 373-6's 73 layers, appropriate for a plan-view-only, single-discipline (architectural, no MEP/structural/civil/landscape) schematic generator at this stage — not a claim of matching 373-6's full layer depth, which would require the not-yet-built modules above (e.g. a real `A-COLS`/structural layer needs `elevation.py`/`section.py` or actual structural input this library doesn't have).

## 6. Did the extended multi-level pipeline work end-to-end?

**Yes.** `LevelSpec` list (6 levels) → `generate_multilevel_plan()` (stacks elevations, calls `compute_footprint()`/`compute_footprint_fitted()` + `build_level_geometry()` per level, all reused unmodified from the single-level pass) → `render_dxf()`/`render_svg()` per level (6 calls, `level=` parameter already supported this) → `export_dwg.export_dwg()` per level (6 calls) → `verify_compliance_multilevel()` (one call across all 6 files) → `titleblock.scan_dxf_for_stamp_language()` per level (6 calls) all ran without needing any workaround. One real bug was caught and fixed mid-pass by the compliance-verification step itself (see §1's max_height re-derivation) — exactly the kind of thing this mandatory pass exists to catch, and a concrete demonstration that it isn't a rubber-stamp step. One cosmetic-but-real issue was also caught and fixed before finalizing: the per-sheet SVG/DXF info-line text originally juxtaposed the whole-building max height (17.25m, includes the roof piyes) directly against the eave-only gabari limit (14.65m) in a way that read as a false violation to a human looking at the sheet, even though the actual `verify_compliance_multilevel()` logic was always correct (it separates eave-height from roof-ridge checks properly) — `plan.py`'s `render_dxf()`/`render_svg()` info-line wording was corrected to state each level's own top elevation plus an explicit pointer to the compliance report, rather than a raw, out-of-context number next to a limit it isn't actually being checked against.

3D rendering (Blender + OpenArt) remains out of scope for this pass, same as the single-level pass — not silently dropped, just not requested for this specific build.

## 7. Door/window schedules (new pass, same day)

**What was built**: `lib/cadgen/schedule.py` — a new library module (not project-specific code) that reads `Building.openings` directly and produces one Door Schedule + one Window Schedule table per level, as DXF (authoritative) + SVG (quick-review), keyed by the same `Opening.mark` labels plan.py already draws on the corresponding plan sheet. `verify_schedule_fidelity()` mirrors `plan.py`'s `verify_compliance()` design intent: it re-reads a just-generated schedule DXF fresh from disk (not the in-memory `Building`) and checks every drawn row against the source `Building`'s own opening data. Every string the module draws (table titles, column headers, cell values, notes, disclaimer) is screened through `titleblock.assert_no_stamp_language()` before being added to a drawing — validated in `lib/cadgen/examples/synthetic_lot_demo.py` (new sections 7a–7d), including a deliberately-broken variant that proves the fidelity checker actually catches a real mismatch (a +0.35m width drift on mark D1), not just that it passes on clean input. Full regression suite: **16/16 checks pass, exit 0** (up from 12/12 before this pass — 4 new schedule-specific checks added, nothing existing broken).

**Applied to 377/1's real output — not synthetic-only.** This required solving a real problem first: no generation script for the multi-level pass was ever saved to disk, only its DXF/SVG/DWG outputs (`cad/377-1-*.{dxf,svg,dwg}`) were persisted — there was no in-memory `Building` object left to hand to `schedule.py`. Rather than guess at the openings or skip 377/1 application, this pass:

1. **Reconstructed** the exact `LevelSpec` list (lot/zoning/room-program/floor-to-floor-height/footprint-style/door-width inputs) from this project's own already-published, source-cited numbers in `brief.md` and this rationale (§1–§4) — nothing new was invented; every input traces to the same citations already in §1.
2. **Regenerated** the Building via `generate_multilevel_plan()` with those inputs.
3. **Cross-validated** the regenerated geometry against the already-shipped, already-compliance-verified plan DXFs, per level, before trusting it: footprint area, footprint bounding box, room count, room left-to-right ordering (name-normalized, since MTEXT paragraph encoding differs cosmetically), and every door/window width as an order-independent multiset. **Result: exact match on every one of these checks, on all 6 levels** (see the session's validation script output — footprint areas matched to the DXF's own floating-point precision, e.g. zemin kat 127.0588 m² both sides, bodrum kat 105.8824 m² both sides; every door/window width matched exactly, e.g. normal kat window widths `[1.09, 1.2, 1.2, 1.2, 1.2]` both sides). Window widths are provably order-independent in this generator (`build_level_geometry()`'s bay width for room *i* depends only on that room's own share of the level's total target area, not on the room's position in the program list), so this cross-validation is a genuine correctness check, not a coincidence of the specific room ordering chosen.
4. Only after that exact match did this pass generate the real schedule sheets — one per level, 6 total — into `cad/`, run `verify_schedule_fidelity()` and the stamp-language audit against each, and confirm **all pass** before calling it done.

## 9. Multi-unit apartment building pivot (this pass) — design-judgment write-up

**Supersedes/extends §1–8 above, does not discard them.** The founder's brief for this pass asked for one final consolidated CAD set matching the founder's own 373-6 reference building's depth (plans + elevations + parking + calculations, not just floor plans), and explicitly asked that the team understand *why* the building is the way it is: what the core is, where it should sit, how many apartments per floor, and whether a reverse duplex (ground + basement) is workable. This section answers that plainly, in normal language, before the numbers.

### 9.1 What changed: from one house to six apartments

The single-level and single-family passes (§1–8) treated 377/1 as one household occupying the whole 5-level building. This pass changes that: **the building is now a small apartment building — a shared vertical core (stair + elevator) serving 6 independent 2+1 apartments, 2 per floor across 3 upper floors**, with a shared entry lobby at grade and parking/service in the basement. Every dimension below traces to `clients/377-1/scripts/generate_377_1.py` (the actual generation script now saved and run, see §10) and to the plan notes citations below.

### 9.2 What the "core" is, and why it goes where it does

The **core** is the building's one shared vertical circulation shaft — the stair plus the elevator (mandatory once total floor count reaches the threshold in plan notes item 4.2.55 **[CORRECTION, 2026-08-21: this citation is wrong — 4.2.55 is actually the elevator-shaft-size minimum (≥3.00 m² asansör boşluğu), already correctly cited for that purpose in `brief.md`'s zemin kat section and in §16.6 below, not a floor-count-threshold rule. The actual mandatory-elevator-at-Kat-Adedi≥4 threshold is Madde 34 of the national Planlı Alanlar İmar Yönetmeliği, already correctly cited in §1 and §3 above.]**, since this building has 5 occupied/serviced floors including the basement) — that every apartment reaches through, instead of each apartment or each floor inventing its own access. A real reinforced-concrete building needs this shaft to land at the *exact same x/y position on every single floor*, because it physically punches straight through every floor slab; it can't shift sideways from one level to the next.

That's a real constraint the design had to satisfy, and a real bug the previous pass didn't catch (documented in `lib/cadgen/plan.py`'s `CoreSpec` docstring): once actually inspected, the earlier per-level-independent generation had drawn a circulation room on the ground floor sheet and a *differently positioned* one upstairs, with **no shaft at all** on the basement or roof sheets — i.e., no continuous vertical shaft existed anywhere in the drawing set, even though the plan sheets individually looked fine in isolation. This pass fixes that with `CoreSpec` + `generate_multilevel_plan(core=...)`: one 3.00 m × 2.60 m shaft, anchored to the same corner of the buildable envelope (front-setback line, left-side-setback line) on every level, verified this pass by `verify_core_alignment()` — which re-reads every level's own DXF fresh from disk and checks the shaft's drawn bounding box is bit-for-bit identical across all 6 sheets (12/12 checks pass, see §10.3).

**Why that corner specifically (front-left, near the street):** three reasons, in order of how load-bearing each one is —
1. It's the only anchor point that's *guaranteed* identical on every level regardless of how each level's own footprint is sized (see §4's earlier discussion of why each level's footprint differs) — `compute_footprint()`/`compute_footprint_fitted()`/`compute_footprint_explicit_depth()` all anchor a footprint's front-left corner at the same setback-derived point no matter its own width/depth. Any other corner (e.g. centered, or rear-anchored) would need every level to also agree on a full width/depth, which they currently don't (a documented, standing simplification — see §4).
2. It puts the elevator/stair immediately inside the main entrance — the entry door (on the ground floor, `entry_at_core_front=True`) opens directly into the core/lobby, which is the standard, legible arrangement for a small apartment building: arrive from the street, see the stair/elevator immediately, don't have to walk past someone else's unit to find it.
3. It keeps the core out of the deep rear yard, where the setback banking already puts most of the building's unused buildable depth (10–11 m of slack rear setback on most levels, see §3's table) — there's no benefit to spending the core's footprint on that end.

### 9.3 Why 2 apartments per floor, not 1 or 3+

This is a real design tradeoff, not an arbitrary number, and it comes down to the KAKS budget (floor-area cap) and what a floor plate that size can actually hold without producing unusable sliver rooms:

- The site's KAKS is 1.00 → max total construction 713.20 m² across whichever levels count toward it. This design counts the ground floor + the 3 normal kat floors (684.46 m² used, ratio **0.9597** — see §10.3), leaving only ~28.7 m² of headroom, well under a 5th similar-sized floor.
- Each normal kat floor is 30.02 m wide × 5.70 m deep (171.11 m² footprint) — a depth chosen specifically so that 4 above-grade floors of this size fit inside the KAKS budget with only a small margin, not a large one. Deepening the floor plate to comfortably fit 3 apartments per floor would push KAKS over 1.00 across 4 floors; the alternative — 3 shallower apartments per floor at the *same* depth — would slice each unit's own rooms into the "sliver" shape the department's own layout algorithm is already known to be prone to (single-row bay-slicing, flagged repeatedly since the single-level pass), producing bedrooms/living rooms too narrow to be believable.
- 2 apartments per floor, once the shared core (2.60 m) and a corridor (1.20 m) are subtracted from the 5.70 m depth, leaves each unit's own rooms in roughly the same believable proportions the rest of this project's plan sheets already use elsewhere (not slivers) — confirmed by actually looking at the generated numbers, not assumed: each unit's Salon comes out ~21 m² (`377-1-1-normal-kat.dxf`, etc.), not a corridor-shaped remainder.
- **1 apartment per floor** was also considered and rejected on economic, not code, grounds: it would under-use the KAKS-affordable floor area (the same footprint, half as many billable apartments) and only yield 3 total dwelling units for the whole building instead of 6 — a materially worse outcome for a capital project whose entire point is dwelling-unit yield on the parcel. It *would* free up more basement depth for a reverse duplex (see §9.4) — that tradeoff is named plainly here, not hidden, but this pass's judgment is that halving total unit count to make room for one duplex unit is not obviously a better use of the site, and is the founder's call to make, not this department's to assume.
- **3+ apartments per floor** was rejected because, at this site's KAKS budget, it can't be done without either exceeding KAKS (deepen the floor plate) or degrading every unit's own room proportions into slivers (keep the same footprint, subdivide further) — a real site/program conflict, not a preference.

### 9.4 The reverse-duplex question (ground + basement) — verdict: conditionally feasible, NOT built as geometry this pass

The founder specifically asked whether apartments spanning the ground floor and basement (a "reverse duplex" — normally a duplex stacks bedroom-over-living, this would stack living-over-basement) are workable here. Short answer: **the plan notes actively contemplate exactly this building type and do not prohibit it, but this specific building's current program has no slack left to build one without displacing something else that is already at its own minimum.** Both halves of that sentence matter — this is a real, cited conditional verdict, not a hunch.

**Why it's not prohibited — the regulation actually invites it:**
- Plan notes item 4.2.1 explicitly excludes from KAKS/emsal "the parts of a basement floor that connect to a ground-floor bağımsız bölüm [independent unit] and do NOT form a separate independent unit of their own" — i.e. a basement level used as the *lower half* of a ground-floor apartment, not sold/titled separately, is free floor area from a KAKS standpoint. This is precisely a reverse duplex.
- Plan notes item 4.2.59 goes further and is the single most important citation for feasibility: normally a habitable basement room needs a real window and can only be buried ≤0.90 m below grade. But **if the basement is arranged as a piyes connected to the ground floor, and the living room plus at least one bedroom of that unit are placed AT the ground floor** (satisfying the normal daylight/room-size minimums there), **the ≤0.90 m burial-depth rule stops applying to that unit's own basement rooms.** In plain terms: put the living room and a bedroom upstairs at grade where they can get real windows, and the regulation lets the rest of that same apartment (extra bedrooms, a rec room, storage) sit in the basement without needing a lightwell or window at all.
- Item 4.2.41 **[CORRECTION, 2026-08-21: this citation is wrong — 4.2.41 is actually the hallway/Hol-width rule (≥1.20 m, Madde 29(3) agrees), already correctly cited for that purpose in `brief.md`'s upper-floor room table; it does not name duplex units. See §16.6 below for the corrected citation set (4.2.37 alone supports this claim) and for where 4.2.41 is correctly applied in the current pass.]** and item 4.2.37 both name "dubleks" (duplex) units explicitly as a recognized building type in this exact plan notes document, including one that reaches into the basement ("...merdiven evlerinin çatıya ve bodruma ulaştırılması zorunlu olmayıp...") — this isn't a workaround the department is inventing, it's a case the local regulation already writes rules for.
- Item 4.2.60 confirms the basement-floor-count math already used in this project (1 habitable basement floor, on top of the above-grade Kat Adedi, without reducing upper floors) already covers exactly one duplex-style basement level — no additional floor-count penalty would be triggered by converting part of the existing basement to duplex use.

**Why it's not built as geometry this pass — three real, concrete conflicts, not a vague "needs more time":**
1. **The ground floor currently has no apartment at all to attach a basement half to.** Zemin kat's entire footprint is shared circulation (Giriş Lobisi + Yönetim Depo Odası) — necessary because this is a 6-unit building with a mandatory shared lobby/core, not incidental. Carving out even one real ground-floor apartment unit would need to either shrink the lobby (workable in principle, needs real space planning not attempted this pass) or grow the ground floor's own footprint — and TAKS is already at 0.2399 of a 0.2500 limit (§10.3), leaving only ~7 m² of coverage headroom, tight for a full apartment.
2. **The basement's parking already fits with zero spare capacity.** This pass confirmed (§11) the basement's Kapalı Otopark room provides exactly 6 stalls against a requirement of exactly 6 (1 per daire × 6 daire) — `provided == required`, no slack. Carving duplex living space out of that same room's footprint would drop the provided stall count below 6 unless the basement footprint is enlarged further (it's already been deepened once this pass, from 10.90 m to 11.70 m, to make the parking fit at all — see §10.2) or the parking is relocated, neither of which was attempted this pass.
3. **Feasibility genuinely depends on real site slope data this project doesn't have.** Item 4.2.59's relief only removes the burial-depth rule for the specific rooms placed correctly; it doesn't change the fact that a reverse-duplex's basement-level rooms still need to be built against the site's *real* grade, not the placeholder 0.00 m kırmızı kot datum this whole project still uses (see §1, unchanged this pass — still no plankote document). Confirming a reverse duplex actually works at this specific site (as opposed to being permitted by the regulation in principle) needs real elevation data this project has flagged as missing since the very first pass.

**Verdict, stated plainly for the team**: reverse duplex units are a real, code-recognized option here, not a dead end — but building one in the current 6-unit/6-required-stall program means either giving up some parking capacity, shrinking the shared lobby, growing the ground-floor footprint toward its TAKS ceiling, or reducing the total apartment count (which circles back to the 1-vs-2-units-per-floor tradeoff in §9.3) — real, competing design moves for the founder to weigh, not a code obstacle. This department did not pick one of those tradeoffs unilaterally this pass; flagged here for the founder's decision, with the exact regulatory support (4.2.1, 4.2.37, 4.2.41 **[CORRECTED — see §16.6: 4.2.41 does not actually support this claim, it is the hallway-width rule; 4.2.37 alone carries the "duplex is a named building type" citation]**, 4.2.59, 4.2.60) needed to act on it whenever that decision is made.

## 16. Maximized-footprint pass — reverse-duplex + roof-duplex, 7 units (this pass)

**Date: 2026-08-21.** Companion to the current `scripts/generate_377_1.py` and the `cad/377-1-*.{dxf,svg,dwg}` set it regenerates. Supersedes §9–§15's 6-unit, all-standalone-apartment program as the project's *current* design, but does not discard those sections — the 6-unit design remains a legitimate, separately-verified checkpoint in this project's history (the script's own docstring says so explicitly), and §9's still-valid reasoning (why the core sits front-left, why 2 apartments per floor, the reverse-duplex feasibility research) continues to apply and is built on here, not repeated from scratch. The CAD regeneration and verification run this section documents was already completed and independently confirmed outside this documentation pass; this section's job is to explain what changed and why, in plain language, sourced directly from the generation script's own design-reasoning comments (`scripts/generate_377_1.py`, section 2) plus the confirmed verification results (§16.7). **Schematic output only — requires licensed-architect review before permitting or construction; not stamped, not code-compliant in a legal sense, no governmental review implied. Internal capital project, not a third-party client engagement** (see `../CLAUDE.md`).

### 16.1 What changed, in plain language

The building now has **7 apartments instead of 6**. Six of the seven are the same idea as the prior pass: 2 ordinary single-floor apartments, unchanged, on the 1st normal kat, and 2 more, unchanged, on the 2nd normal kat. What's new:

- **The ground floor gains one apartment.** Previously the entire zemin kat was shared circulation (entry lobby + a management/storage room) — no apartment at all lived there. Now, alongside that same shared lobby, one apartment ("Ters Dubleks 1" / reverse duplex) occupies part of the ground floor *and* part of the basement below it, linked by its own private internal stair that only that apartment uses.
- **The top floor's 2 apartments each grow downward from the roof instead of staying single-floor.** The 3rd normal kat still has the same 2 apartments it always did, but each one now also owns a private slice of the çatı piyesi (roof-level room) above it, reached by its own internal stair ("Çatı Dubleks 1" and "Çatı Dubleks 2"). This doesn't create new apartments — it makes 2 existing ones bigger.

Net change: **+1 apartment**, entirely from the new ground-floor/basement unit — for a total of **7 dwelling units**, up from 6.

### 16.2 Why 7, not 6 or 8

The arithmetic, straight from the generation script: 1 (reverse duplex) + 2 (1st normal kat, unchanged) + 2 (2nd normal kat, unchanged) + 2 (roof duplex) = 7.

- **Why not 6 (why bother with the reverse duplex at all):** the ground floor had spare, code-eligible capacity sitting unused — see §16.3 for exactly why the basement can legally attach to it — and turning that into one more apartment directly serves this project's purpose as a capital project (dwelling-unit yield on the parcel), the same logic §9.3 already used to justify 2 apartments per floor over 1.
- **Why not 8 (a 2nd reverse-duplex unit):** ruled out by a real, quantified parking-width conflict, not a preference — see §16.3.
- **Why the roof-duplex move doesn't add a 3rd/4th extra unit on top of the reverse duplex's +1:** converting the 3rd floor's 2 apartments into 2 roof duplexes is unit-count-neutral by construction — see §16.4. It makes those 2 apartments bigger, not more numerous.
- **Why the 1st and 2nd normal kat weren't also converted to duplexes:** each of those floors only borders another full apartment floor above and below it (1st borders zemin below and 2nd above; 2nd borders 1st below and 3rd above) — there's no adjacent "spare" level to duplex into, unlike the ground floor (which borders the basement, previously pure service space) or the 3rd floor (which borders the çatı piyesi, previously undifferentiated roof allowance). This is a straightforward reading of the level stack itself, not a separate design choice the script comments call out explicitly.

### 16.3 The reverse-duplex unit: bodrum + zemin, capped at exactly 1

**What it is:** one apartment, "Ters Dubleks 1," with its living room, kitchen, and one bedroom (Salon 22.0 m², Mutfak 10.0 m², Yatak Odası 1 13.0 m²) at the ground floor, and its remaining rooms — a second bedroom, bathroom, and a study (Yatak Odası 2 11.0 m², Banyo WC 5.0 m², Çalışma Odası 8.0 m²) — one level down in the basement, reached only by this unit's own private internal stair. Putting the living room and a bedroom at grade is not incidental — it's what plan notes item 4.2.59 requires before the basement rooms of a duplex like this are allowed to skip the normal habitable-basement window/burial-depth rule (already established in §9.4, unaffected by this pass).

**Why capped at 1 unit, not 2 — a real, quantified conflict, not a design preference:** the basement's own interior width is about 29.77 m. Parking for the whole building now needs 7 stalls (1 per apartment, Otopark Yönetmeliği Ek-1 — up from 6, because there are now 7 apartments), which needs about 17.5 m of that width. One reverse-duplex unit's own zone needs 9.0 m. Together those already use nearly all of the basement's width, leaving only about 0.2 m spare — nowhere near the ~9 m a second reverse-duplex unit would need. Fitting a second unit would require either shrinking it below Madde 29's mandatory minimum room dimensions (not acceptable — a national code minimum, not a target to shave) or a double-loaded/stacked parking layout, which `lib/cadgen/parking.py` doesn't support yet (single-loaded row only, a documented limitation — see §11). Both are real, named follow-ups, not vague "future work" — but neither was attempted this pass, so the reverse duplex stays at exactly 1 unit.

### 16.4 The roof-duplex units: 3rd normal kat + çatı piyesi, unit-count-neutral by design

**What it is:** both of the 3rd normal kat's existing apartments ("Çatı Dubleks 1" and "Çatı Dubleks 2") keep their full apartment program at the 3rd floor (Salon, Mutfak, 2 Yatak Odası, Banyo WC) and each additionally gains one private room in the çatı piyesi above (Çatı Odası, 22.0 m², a flex/loft room), reached by its own internal stair. The piyesi's separate mechanical/solar equipment room (Mekanik Solar Ekipman Odası, 6.0 m²) stays exactly what it was — shared building infrastructure, not attached to either apartment.

**Why this is unit-count-neutral:** no new apartment is created on the 3rd floor or the roof — the same 2 apartments that existed before simply get bigger, by claiming space (the çatı piyesi) that was already part of the building's program but previously undifferentiated ("a small roof terrace plus mechanical/solar equipment enclosure," not assigned to any unit). That's why this move contributes 0 to the +1 net unit change described in §16.1/§16.2 — the entire gain comes from the reverse duplex alone.

**A real, accepted tradeoff, stated plainly:** because a duplex unit's stair has to land at the identical footprint position on both of its levels, the 3rd normal kat and the çatı piyesi were both narrowed to the same matched width (27.0 m, versus the ~30.02 m full envelope width every other KAKS-counted level uses) so the pair lines up. That gives up 17.91 m² of KAKS-countable floor area on the 3rd floor (178.02 m² → 160.11 m²) compared to using the full envelope width there — a real, quantified cost of building the roof duplex honestly (with genuine cross-level alignment, verified by `verify_duplex_stair_alignment()`, §16.7) rather than fudging it.

### 16.5 How the footprint maximization worked

Separately from the duplex moves, this pass pushed the building's footprint itself outward, toward the TAKS ceiling, rather than keeping the smaller, schematic-sized footprint the prior 6-unit pass used. The ground floor's depth (and, replicated identically, each of the 3 normal kat floors' depth) was set to 5.93 m — still anchored at the same front-setback and side-setback lines as every prior pass (§3's setback table is unchanged) — producing a footprint of 178.02 m² against the TAKS-derived cap of 178.32 m² (713.26 m² × 0.25): a TAKS ratio of 0.2496, 99.84% of the legal maximum, with a small deliberate safety margin rather than shaving it to the exact limit. The basement's own depth (11.70 m) is unchanged from the prior pass — it was already validated against real parking geometry and isn't TAKS/KAKS-constrained, so there was no reason to touch it.

One nuance worth flagging plainly: this does **not** fully resolve the standing "per-level independent footprint, not one true structural footprint" limitation already flagged in §4/§8/§15. Zemin, 1st, and 2nd normal kat now genuinely share one identical footprint (full envelope width × 5.93 m depth) — but the 3rd normal kat and çatı piyesi are narrower (27.0 m matched width, §16.4), and the basement is deeper and differently shaped again (11.70 m depth, full width). So the building is *more* consistent floor-to-floor than before, but still not one single stacked structural footprint top to bottom — a real structural engineer would still need to reconcile this, same standing caveat as before.
