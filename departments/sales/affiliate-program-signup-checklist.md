## Niche 1: Carbide-tipped / machining tools

### Amazon Associates — Tools & Home Improvement category

- **Commission**: ~3% (Amazon's "Tools & Home Improvement" / general home-improvement bucket sits in its
  standard low-tier category rate; multiple third-party rate-card summaries agree on 3%, but Amazon's own
  official rate card page requires a logged-in Associates account to view, so this is a secondary-source
  confirmation, not a primary one).
- **Cookie window**: 24 hours (Amazon's standard, not category-specific) — short relative to every
  fragrance-dupe program below.
- **Network**: in-house (Amazon's own program, not run through ShareASale/CJ/Impact/Rakuten).
- **Application requirements**: requires a **live, functioning site or app** with original content —
  guidance from multiple sources converges on roughly 10+ pieces of genuine original content as a
  practical minimum before applying; "under construction" or thin placeholder sites are a common rejection
  reason. After approval, Amazon requires **3 qualifying sales within the first 180 days** of signup or
  the account is closed and has to be re-applied for — the clock starts the moment the application is
  submitted, so timing the application to when the site can realistically drive traffic matters, not just
  when it's "live."
- **Tax/payout**: non-US applicants (Sirketim is Turkey-based) file a **W-8BEN** (individual) or
  **W-8BEN-E** (business entity) tax interview in Associates Central; without a valid treaty claim, Amazon
  withholds up to 30% of US-sourced commission income. Payout minimums: $10 for direct deposit, $100 for
  check (plus a $15 check-processing fee) — direct deposit is the only practical option at small-affiliate
  scale.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: **after.** This is the single hardest-gated program in either
  niche — live site with real content is a precondition, not a formality, and the 180-day sales clock
  makes premature application actively costly (an account can get closed for missing the 3-sale bar before
  the site has any real traffic).

### MSC Industrial Supply

- **Commission**: 0.8% of mscdirect.com purchases — confirmed via FlexOffers listing. This is lower than
  Amazon's 3%, which is a meaningful finding: MSC Industrial is not the good deal category-name
  recognition would suggest.
- **Cookie window**: 7 days.
- **Network**: FlexOffers.
- **Application requirements**: FlexOffers reviews applications on website functionality, product/service
  fit, and navigation quality; approval typically 24-48 hours for pre-approved programs, up to 2 weeks for
  restricted ones. Not explicitly confirmed whether MSC's specific listing requires a fully live site vs.
  accepting an in-progress one — FlexOffers' general review criteria (site functionality, navigation)
  implies a working site is expected.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: after — FlexOffers' review criteria centers on an actual
  functioning site, and given the very low payout (0.8%), this program is a low priority regardless of
  sequencing.

### W.W. Grainger (listed as "Grainger Global Online Business")

- **Commission**: ~5% per sale (some listings cite a 5-10% range — not fully consistent across sources).
- **Cookie window**: 30 days.
- **Network**: not confirmed via this research pass — multiple affiliate-directory listings (TapRefer,
  FlexOffers, viglink/Sovrn Commerce) surface Grainger's program without agreeing on or clearly stating
  which single network hosts it; it may be listed redundantly across more than one directory/aggregator
  rather than natively on all of them. Verify the actual host network directly on Grainger's own site at
  application time.
- **Application requirements**: not confirmed beyond the generic "basic business/audience info" pattern
  common to these directory listings.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: after — insufficient confirmed detail to say otherwise, and every
  comparable industrial-supply program found in this pass gates on a working site.

### Global Industrial (Global Equipment Company)

- **Commission**: 4% per sale.
- **Cookie window**: 30 days.
- **Network**: CJ Affiliate (Commission Junction) — confirmed.
- **Application requirements**: CJ requires the applicant to own a live website (18+ age requirement
  also applies); each advertiser (Global Industrial here) then reviews the applicant's site individually
  after CJ-network-level acceptance, typically 2-3 days for well-managed programs. Two-layer approval:
  CJ network account first, then the specific Global Industrial program.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: after — CJ's network-level gate alone requires a live site before
  an account can even be opened, before Global Industrial's own program review happens.

### KC Tool (via Refersion) — direct manufacturer/retailer program, not KBC Tools

- Note: the brief's example "KBC Tools" does not appear to run an active affiliate program — searches
  across major networks (Awin, Pepperjam, Conversant, Rakuten) turned up nothing for KBC Tools & Machinery.
  **KC Tool** (kctool.com, a premium hand-tool retailer, distinct from KBC) does have a real, currently
  live program and is the closer direct-retailer match found in this pass.
- **Commission**: up to 10% on referred sales.
- **Cookie window**: 30 days.
- **Network**: Refersion (kctool.refersion.com).
- **Application requirements**: Refersion is merchant-controlled, not network-gated the way
  ShareASale/CJ/Rakuten are — each merchant sets its own approval bar and some Refersion merchants
  auto-approve applicants. KC Tool's own page doesn't publish explicit traffic/content minimums, only
  "join and start earning." This makes it the **most likely of all niche-1 programs found to accept an
  early-stage or newly-live site** — worth testing first once any site exists, even a thin one, since the
  downside of a rejected application is low.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: after the site exists at all (even minimally) — but this is the
  one program in this niche that plausibly doesn't need substantial traffic first, unlike the network-gated
  programs above.

### MRO-focused affiliate networks generally

No dedicated MRO-only affiliate network (distinct from the general networks above) was found in this
research pass. MSC Industrial, Grainger, and Global Industrial — the three major MRO/industrial distributors
searched — each run their affiliate program through a general-purpose network (FlexOffers, unconfirmed, and
CJ respectively) rather than a specialist MRO affiliate network. If a dedicated MRO affiliate network exists,
it wasn't surfaced by this search pass and should be treated as unconfirmed, not absent.

## Niche 2: Niche/indie perfume "dupes" and comparison content

### Scentbird

- **Commission**: up to 14% per subscription referred — confirmed, matches existing
  `affiliate-niche-research.md` figure.
- **Cookie window**: 45 days — confirmed, matches existing doc.
- **Network**: Impact (impact.com) — confirmed directly (Scentbird runs no in-house affiliate software).
- **Application requirements**: free to join, application form on Scentbird's site routes through Impact.
  Impact's own network-level applicant guidance commonly cited elsewhere suggests roughly 5,000-10,000+
  monthly visitors as a rule of thumb for network applications generally, though this is a general Impact
  norm, not a Scentbird-specific published minimum — Scentbird's own page doesn't state a hard traffic
  floor.
- **Payout**: monthly, $50 minimum to withdraw, via bank transfer/ACH.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: after — Impact is one of the stricter major networks on applicant
  quality, and $50/month payout minimum plus Impact's general traffic norms both favor waiting until the
  site has real visits.

### Dossier

- **Commission**: **conflicting figures found, not resolved this pass.** `affiliate-niche-research.md`
  states "up to ~20%"; this research pass found sources citing 15% (via ShareASale) and separately 5% —
  the discrepancy is large enough (5% vs 15% vs 20%) that it should be treated as unconfirmed until
  checked directly against Dossier's own ShareASale-hosted program page at actual application time, not
  relied on for planning.
- **Cookie window**: 30 days (this figure was consistent across sources, unlike commission).
- **Network**: ShareASale — confirmed.
- **Application requirements**: ShareASale's network-level bar is explicit and strict: a live website
  with a custom (non-subdomain) domain, an About page, English-language content, and "good quality content
  that gives value to readers" driving consistent traffic. ShareASale explicitly will not approve a site
  that's under development or has technical issues. Review takes 5-7 business days at the network level,
  separate from Dossier's own program-level approval.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: after — ShareASale's stated requirements are the most explicit
  "must be genuinely live with real content" bar found across all programs in this research pass.

### MicroPerfumes

- **Commission**: ~15% (confirmed for travel-size products specifically; full-size commission not
  separately confirmed) — roughly matches existing doc's "~15%."
- **Cookie window**: 30 days (not previously noted in `affiliate-niche-research.md` — new detail from
  this pass).
- **Network**: Rakuten Advertising (formerly Rakuten Marketing/LinkShare) — confirmed; existing doc didn't
  specify a network for this program.
- **Application requirements**: Rakuten's network-level bar mirrors ShareASale's — applicant must submit
  a **live (not under-construction)** website, blog, or "customized" social page with unique, quality
  content adding real value; review takes a few days. After Rakuten network approval, MicroPerfumes'
  specific program has its own separate approval step with potentially additional criteria.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: after — same "live, not under-construction" language as
  ShareASale.

### FragranceX

- **Commission**: 10% per sale on standard affiliate traffic — this pass found 10%, not the existing
  doc's "~8%"; the doc should be updated. Note a caveat found alongside this figure: coupon/loyalty-site
  affiliates are paid a lower 1-4% tier, which doesn't apply to a content/comparison site model.
- **Cookie window**: 45 days — this is new detail; the existing doc doesn't currently state a cookie
  window for FragranceX.
- **Network**: CJ Affiliate (Commission Junction) — confirmed; also listed via FlexOffers as a secondary
  route.
- **Application requirements**: same two-layer CJ pattern as Global Industrial above — CJ network account
  first (requires a live, owned website, 18+), then FragranceX's own program-level review.
- **Apply now vs. after site is live**: after — CJ's network-level live-site requirement applies here too.

### Cross-cutting finding for niche 2

Every dedicated fragrance program found in this pass — Scentbird (Impact), Dossier (ShareASale),
MicroPerfumes (Rakuten), FragranceX (CJ) — sits on one of the four major general-purpose affiliate
networks, and **all four of those networks independently state or strongly imply a live, non-under-construction
site with genuine content is required before an applicant account is even approved at the network level**,
before the individual brand's own program review happens. This is a real, confirmed constraint, not
speculation — ShareASale and Rakuten both explicitly use "not under construction" language.

## Combined summary: earliest realistic point to start getting real links

Given today's actual state (no domain, no live site, no content for either niche):

1. **Every dedicated/brand-level program in both niches, on every major network found (Impact, ShareASale,
   CJ, Rakuten, and Amazon's own in-house program), requires a live website with genuine content before an
   application can succeed** — not just as a soft preference but as an explicitly stated or strongly
   implied gate at the network level, separate from and prior to the individual brand's own review. No
   program found in either niche offers a genuine "apply before the site exists" path, contrary to what a
   generic "some programs accept under-construction sites" assumption might suggest — that assumption did
   not hold up in this research pass for either niche.
2. **The one plausible partial exception**: KC Tool's Refersion-hosted program (niche 1) is
   merchant-controlled rather than network-gated the way ShareASale/CJ/Rakuten/Impact are, and its own
   page states no explicit traffic minimum — it's worth testing as soon as a niche-1 site exists at all,
   even minimally, since Refersion merchants can and do auto-approve. This is the earliest realistic
   "real link" opportunity found across both niches, but it is a smaller, lower-commission program (up to
   10%, one retailer), not a substitute for the higher-value programs.
3. **Sequencing implication for both niches is the same**: site-live-with-real-content has to come first,
   for every program that matters commercially (Amazon Associates for niche 1; Scentbird/Dossier/FragranceX
   for niche 2). Applying earlier doesn't unlock anything — it either gets auto-rejected (network-level
   gate) or, in Amazon's specific case, actively starts a 180-day countdown to 3 qualifying sales that's
   better not started before the site can realistically drive traffic.
4. **Practical order once a site goes live**: apply to the network-hosted programs (ShareASale/CJ/Impact/
   Rakuten registration + the specific brand program) once there's a real content base — the "~10 posts"
   rule of thumb Amazon-focused guidance converges on is a reasonable proxy for "real content" across
   these other networks too, since their stated bar (quality content, real traffic) is similar in spirit
   even where the exact numeric threshold isn't published. Amazon Associates specifically should be the
   last application submitted of the batch, precisely because of the 180-day/3-sale clock — apply once
   there's already some traffic, not on day one of going live.
5. **What's unresolved and needs re-checking at actual application time, not before**: Grainger's actual
   host network (unconfirmed), Dossier's actual commission rate (conflicting 5%/15%/20% figures found),
   and whether any dedicated MRO-specific affiliate network exists beyond the general-purpose networks
   found here.
