[By Kilian Angels' Share](/fragrance/angels-share/) is a Woody Oriental with one of the shortest pyramids in our catalogue: **cognac** on top, **cinnamon, tonka bean and oak** in the heart, **sandalwood, vanilla and oak wood** underneath. Four of those notes are sweet or warm, and the one that makes it distinctive is the first.

That matters for anyone shopping for an alternative, because it tells you what to look for and what you can safely ignore.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Cognac |
| Heart | Cinnamon, Tonka Bean, Oak |
| Base | Sandalwood, Vanilla, Oak Wood |

In our catalogue it rates **8/10 for longevity** and **7/10 for sillage**, with a freshness score of 1 and a sweetness score of 8. It is a dense, warm, dessert-adjacent fragrance, and the oak gives it a drier edge than a plain vanilla.

Cinnamon, tonka and vanilla are common materials in this style. The cognac is the part that is hard to find elsewhere.

## The question to ask of any alternative: does it open on cognac?

Most fragrances sold as cognac or "boozy" lean on spice and sweetness to suggest a drink. A few name the note outright. That one difference separates the alternatives we list.

We list three alternatives for Angels' Share. In each case the pairing is **our editorial judgement**, recorded in the verdict on the listing, not a claim that the two are the same fragrance.

**Royal Blend Extrait by French Avenue** is the only one of the three whose published pyramid opens on cognac. Its heart is iris and myrrh rather than cinnamon and oak, with tonka and vanilla beneath. Our verdict is that on the thing which defines Angels' Share it gets closer than the better-known options, but it is drier, and reads more like a spirit than a pudding.

**Khamrah by Lattafa** sits in the same boozy-cinnamon-praline territory but pushes the dates and spice further. Its listed notes include cinnamon, nutmeg, dates, praline, vanilla and tonka. Our verdict: sweeter and heavier-handed, and without the cognac that gives the Kilian its lift.

**Khamrah Qahwa by Lattafa** is the coffee flanker of Khamrah. The same cinnamon-praline-vanilla spine carries a roasted bitterness over the top. Coffee stands in for some of the cognac's depth, but pulls the sweetness towards breakfast, a direction Angels' Share never goes.

## Why the cognac is the hard part

Cinnamon, vanilla and tonka are easy to dose, and a great many fragrances use them. A named spirit note is a different matter: it is what makes a reader say "boozy" and not merely "sweet and spicy". So when you compare listings, the useful question is not how many notes overlap but whether the thing that defines the original is still present. Overlap in the warm, sweet middle of a pyramid is cheap to achieve, which is also why we avoid treating a high overlap as proof of a good pairing.

## Three ways to choose

**You want the drink.** Start with Royal Blend Extrait. It is the only listing that names cognac, and the trade is a drier, less dessert-like heart.

**You want the dessert.** Khamrah is the closer match for the cinnamon, vanilla and tonka side of Angels' Share, and it leans harder into sweetness. Expect something richer than the original, not a copy.

**You want something to wear in a different way.** Khamrah Qahwa trades the spirit for coffee. It is a reasonable choice if you like the structure and would enjoy a roasted note, and a poor one if you want a cognac fragrance.

## Where claims come from

All three listings carry a pyramid the maker or seller declared, and we have not smelled any of them. Descriptions such as "praline" or "cognac" are the seller's published notes, not our observation. Our own scoring treats those pyramids as declared, not independently checked, which is one reason our published scores are capped. The [methodology is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Compare the opening, not the name.** If the alternative's pyramid does not mention a spirit, it will not smell like one.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size** on the listing, since an extrait and an eau de parfum are different strengths and may not wear alike.
- **Patch-test** any new fragrance on a small area of skin before wearing it widely.
- **Consider a small size first.** These are warm, sweet fragrances, and sweetness reads very differently on different people.
