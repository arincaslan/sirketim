[Hermes Terre d'Hermes](/fragrance/terre-dhermes/) is a Fresh Woody fragrance, sold as an eau de toilette, that pairs sharp citrus with a dry, stony base. Our catalogue puts **grapefruit and orange** on top, **flint, pepper and geranium** in the heart, and **vetiver, cedar, benzoin and oakmoss** in the base.

It rates **6/10 for freshness**, **7/10 for longevity** and **6/10 for sillage**, with low sweetness. Our catalogue lists its longevity at roughly six to eight hours and its sillage as strong. In practice that is a bright citrus start that settles into something mineral, earthy and dry.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Grapefruit, Orange |
| Heart | Flint, Pepper, Geranium |
| Base | Vetiver, Cedar, Benzoin, Oakmoss |

Flint is the unusual one. It is an accord rather than a plant: a struck-stone, slightly smoky mineral impression. Together with pepper and vetiver it is what makes Terre d'Hermes recognisable and not just another citrus woody. A copy that gets the citrus right and the flint wrong will smell like a generic fresh scent.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance, and we would rather say so than pad the page.

**Harmony by AromaPassions** is paired with Terre d'Hermes by the seller, not by us. The product title on the retailer's page reads "Inspired by" Hermes Terre d'Hermes, and that is the seller's claim.

The seller declares **orange, grapefruit, pepper, pelargonium, flint, vetiver, cedar, patchouli and benzoin**. Compared with our catalogue entry:

- **Flint, pepper, vetiver, cedar, orange, grapefruit and benzoin all match**, including the mineral note that defines the original.
- **Pelargonium is geranium.** It is the botanical name for the same material. We keep the seller's wording as published, so a mechanical comparison counts the two as different, and a good share of the gap on paper is vocabulary.
- **The real declared difference is at the close.** The listing adds patchouli and does not declare oakmoss. Patchouli is earthy and slightly sweet, oakmoss is dry and mossy. Our verdict is to expect a slightly sweeter, earthier drydown against the original's drier finish.

One caution about how this listing is described. AromaPassions publishes a single flat list of materials, not a pyramid. The split into top, heart and base on our listing is ours, which is why we make no claims here that depend on which layer a note sits in. We can say what the list includes and what it lacks. We cannot say how it unfolds on skin.

## Why "same notes" does not settle it

- **Concentration is a separate variable.** The original is an eau de toilette. The listing is described by its seller as an extrait de parfum. More concentrated does not mean closer: it can mean a heavier, longer version of a fresh scent that was designed to be bright. What a seller calls a concentration is the seller's description rather than something we have tested.
- **Citrus fades first.** Orange and grapefruit are the quickest materials to leave, so the opening is the least reliable part of any comparison. What you live with for hours is the vetiver, cedar and whatever sits under them.
- **A list does not carry proportions.** Flint in a whisper and flint in a clear dose are the same line on paper.

## How to decide

**You want the stony, peppery Terre character for less, and a slightly earthier finish is fine.** The listed alternative declares the defining materials, so it is worth reading closely.

**You are particular about the dry, mossy finish.** Check that patchouli-for-oakmoss swap before anything else. It is the clearest declared difference.

**You want something in the same space but not a copy.** The originals below take other routes.

## Where claims come from

The notes and facets for Terre d'Hermes are our catalogue's. The note list for Harmony is the one its seller published, and the pairing is the seller's own description. We have not smelled either bottle, and the listing has not been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. [How we score is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Read the full note list.** Look for what the listing adds, here patchouli, and what it omits, here oakmoss.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size** on the product page, and compare like with like before judging value.
- **Be sceptical of marketing claims** about strength or longevity. These are the seller's words, not our findings.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin before you commit to a full bottle.
