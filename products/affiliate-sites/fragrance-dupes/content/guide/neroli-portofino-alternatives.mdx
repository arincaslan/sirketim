[Tom Ford Neroli Portofino](/fragrance/neroli-portofino/) is a Citrus Aromatic eau de toilette, and it is one of the freshest fragrances in our catalogue: **9/10 for freshness**, against **2/10 for sweetness**, **3/10 for warmth** and **2/10 for woodiness**.

Our catalogue puts **neroli, bergamot and lemon** on top, **orange blossom, angelica and lavender** in the heart, and **amber, musk and sandalwood** in the base. Longevity is listed at roughly four to six hours with moderate sillage, which is typical for a citrus-led fragrance and worth knowing before you compare it with anything heavier.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Neroli, Bergamot, Lemon |
| Heart | Orange Blossom, Angelica, Lavender |
| Base | Amber, Musk, Sandalwood |

The structure is that of a classic cologne: a burst of citrus, a floral and herbal middle, and a soft, skin-close base. The neroli is what separates it from a plain lemon-and-bergamot splash. It adds a bitter-sweet floral note that sits between citrus and white flower.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance, and we would rather say so than pad the page.

**Neroli Riviera by Fragrance World** is paired with Neroli Portofino by a retailer. The listing on Opulensi describes it with the words "Inspired by the freshness of Neroli Portofino". That is the seller's claim, not ours.

The seller declares **bergamot, mandarin orange, lemon, bitter orange, lavender, rosemary and myrtle** on top, **African orange flower, neroli, jasmine and pittosporum** in the heart, and **amber, ambrette seed and angelica** in the base.

- **It shares the core.** Bergamot, lemon, neroli, lavender, angelica and amber appear in both lists.
- **It is a busier build.** Rosemary and myrtle add an herbal edge the original does not have, and our verdict says it reads a shade greener.
- **The base differs.** The original's sandalwood and musk are not in the declaration, and ambrette seed takes their place. Our verdict is that this makes it feel drier and less skin-close.

The pyramid is the one its seller published, we have not smelled the bottle, and the listing has not been independently verified. Stock changes, so check the product page for current availability.

## Why a citrus scent is hard to judge from a list

Citrus materials are the lightest and most volatile in perfumery, so they are the first to fade. That is why this style is usually described as short-lived in the first place, and why a copy that matches the opening can still diverge within an hour. What stays behind is the base, and that is exactly where the two lists differ.

For anyone buying for summer, the sensible test is not the first spray. It is **the third hour**: what is left on skin once the lemon and bergamot have gone.

## What neroli actually is

Neroli is the aromatic material from the blossom of the bitter orange tree, which is why it sits so comfortably beside citrus fruit. In this composition it bridges the two halves of the fragrance: it is part of the opening, then the orange blossom carries a related floral note through the middle. A list that names both neroli and an orange flower is describing a closely related family of materials, which helps explain why this one is easy to approximate on paper and harder to match in the wearing.

It also means a buyer who dislikes heavy white florals can usually wear this style comfortably. The floral element is clean and bright, not creamy.

## How to decide

**You want the same bright, neroli-led opening.** The listing shares the outline and is worth reading in full.

**You want the exact soft, musky skin-scent finish.** The declaration swaps it for a drier base.

**You want something fresher or more tropical.** Look at the originals below.

## What to check before you buy

- **Read the full note list** and look at what sits underneath the citrus.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size** on the product page.
- **Be sceptical of strength and longevity claims.** These are the seller's words, not our findings.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin before you commit to a full bottle.
