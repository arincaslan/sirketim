"Dupe" has become a catch-all term for anything sold as an alternative to a
designer fragrance, which is exactly why so many people end up disappointed.
A dupe that matches the top notes but skips the base isn't a bad product,
it's just being marketed as something it isn't. Here's how to actually find
one that does what you want.

## Start from the part you love, not the whole bottle

Most fragrances people want to dupe are memorable for one specific
stretch, usually the opening or the drydown, not the whole four-hour arc.
Before you shop, decide which part matters to you. If it's the first twenty
minutes you're chasing, note overlap in the top notes matters most. If it's
what people notice on you at 6pm after you sprayed at 9am, you care about
the base notes and the concentration, not the opening.

Our <a href="/dupe-finder">dupe finder</a> weights base notes highest by
design, at 45% of the overlap score, specifically because that's the part
that actually lasts through a normal day. If you're shopping for a specific
moment rather than the whole wear, check the facet breakdown, not just the
headline percentage.

## Concentration changes more than you'd think

An Eau de Parfum dupe of an Extrait de Parfum original will almost always
wear lighter and fade faster, even with an identical note list. That's not
the dupe brand cutting corners, extrait is a genuinely more concentrated (and
more expensive to produce) format. Check the concentration before you
compare longevity claims across two products.

## Buy the smallest size first

Every fragrance, dupe or designer, reads differently on different skin
chemistry. A 30ml bottle is a reasonable amount to lose if a dupe doesn't
work out on you specifically. Save the full-size purchase for a scent
you've already tested at a smaller size, on your own skin, for at least a
full day.
