"Dupe" has become a catch-all term for anything sold as an alternative to a
designer fragrance, which is exactly why so many people end up disappointed.
A dupe that matches the top notes but skips the base isn't a bad product,
it's just being marketed as something it isn't. Here's how to actually find
one that does what you want.

## Start from the part you love, not the whole bottle

Most fragrances people want to dupe are memorable for one specific
stretch, usually the opening or the drydown, not the whole four-hour arc.
Before you shop, decide which part matters to you. If it's the first twenty
minutes you're chasing, note overlap in the top notes matters most. If it's
what people notice on you at 6pm after you sprayed at 9am, you care about
the base notes and the concentration, not the opening.

Our <a href="/dupe-finder">dupe finder</a> weights base notes highest by
design, at 45% of the overlap score, specifically because that's the part
that actually lasts through a normal day. If you're shopping for a specific
moment rather than the whole wear, check the facet breakdown, not just the
headline percentage.

## Concentration changes more than you'd think

An Eau de Parfum dupe of an Extrait de Parfum original will almost always
wear lighter and fade faster, even with an identical note list. That's not
the dupe brand cutting corners, extrait is a genuinely more concentrated (and
more expensive to produce) format. Check the concentration before you
compare longevity claims across two products.

## Read the base notes before anything else

If you take one habit from this page, take this one.

A note pyramid is a rough timeline: top notes last five to twenty minutes,
heart notes run to about three hours, and base notes carry the rest of the day.
So the base is where you — and everyone standing near you — spend by far the
most time.

This is why a dupe can match a pyramid on paper and disappoint in practice. A
product that nails a bright bergamot-and-pink-pepper opening and then collapses
into a generic sweet musk has reproduced the twenty minutes you notice least.
It will pass the shop-counter test and fail the workday.

Our formula weights the tiers unequally for exactly this reason — **base 45%,
heart 35%, top 20%** — rather than counting note overlap as a flat list. The
mechanics are in <a href="/guide/how-to-read-a-note-pyramid">how to read a note
pyramid</a>, and the full method is on
<a href="/about#methodology">our methodology page</a>.

## Match the facets, not just the note list

Two fragrances can share ten of twelve listed notes and smell substantially
different, because a list says nothing about **proportion**. One might be
saffron-forward with a whisper of jasmine; the other a jasmine soliflore with a
pinch of saffron.

Facets — sweet, fresh, woody, spicy, powdery — describe how a fragrance *reads*
rather than what went into it, so they survive the proportion problem. When a
match percentage looks high but something feels off, the facets are usually
where the disagreement is.

The same logic applies one level up: check the **family**. A match across a
family boundary rarely feels related, however well the notes line up. See
<a href="/guide/fragrance-families-explained">fragrance families explained</a>.

## Check what it costs per millilitre, not per bottle

A 30ml at €29 and a 100ml at €75 are not the price relationship they look like
at a glance. Per millilitre, the second is cheaper by a third.

This matters more with alternatives than with originals, because dupe houses
sell in a wider spread of sizes and the headline price does a lot of work in
the marketing. Every "Nx cheaper" claim is only meaningful per ml.

## Buy the smallest size first

Every fragrance, dupe or designer, reads differently on different skin
chemistry. A 30ml bottle is a reasonable amount to lose if a dupe doesn't
work out on you specifically. Save the full-size purchase for a scent
you've already tested at a smaller size, on your own skin, for at least a
full day.

Better still, buy a **decant** — a few millilitres from a splitter, usually
€4–12 — and wear it for two full days before deciding anything. Almost every
wasted fragrance purchase is a prediction that could have been an observation.

## The short version

1. **Decide which part of the wear you actually want** — the opening or the
   drydown. They point at different products.
2. **Read the base notes first.** They are most of your day.
3. **Check the facets and the family**, not just the note list.
4. **Compare concentrations** before comparing longevity claims.
5. **Compare price per ml.**
6. **Test small.** A decant, then a 30ml, then a bottle.
