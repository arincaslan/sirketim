[Dior Sauvage](/fragrance/sauvage/) is **$110 for 100ml** — by some distance the cheapest of the three most-duped fragrances, and the one people are most often surprised to see copied at all.

The surprise is the point. Sauvage is not expensive, so an alternative cannot compete on price alone the way it can against [Baccarat Rouge 540](/guide/baccarat-rouge-540-alternatives/) at $4.64/ml or [Aventus](/guide/aventus-alternatives/) at $445 a bottle. It has to compete on something else, and understanding what that is tells you whether buying one makes sense.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Bergamot, Pepper |
| Heart | Sichuan Pepper, Lavender, Pink Pepper, Geranium, Elemi |
| Base | Ambroxan, Cedar, Labdanum |

The base note is the whole story. **Ambroxan** is a synthetic amber material — clean, mineral, slightly salty, enormously powerful, and available to every perfumer on earth at modest cost. Sauvage is essentially a very well-judged bergamot-and-pepper opening sitting on a large dose of it.

This is why alternatives are everywhere. The defining material is not scarce, not restricted and not expensive. Anyone can dose Ambroxan.

## So what does a good one actually get right?

If the base material is freely available, the difference is entirely in the first twenty minutes.

**The pepper has to be bright, not hot.** Sauvage opens with bergamot and three kinds of pepper, and the effect is meant to be sparkling and slightly abrasive rather than warm and spicy. Cheap versions read as generic "fresh," because a convincing peppery citrus opening is one of the more difficult things to build and the Ambroxan will carry the fragrance regardless.

**The lavender has to be there without announcing itself.** It is what keeps Sauvage from smelling like a pure amber-fresh; it also drags a composition toward "old-fashioned barbershop" if it is overdosed. It sits low in the mix on purpose.

**The dose has to be judged.** Sauvage rates **8/10 longevity** and **8/10 sillage** in our catalog, in the Beast Mode band, and it is famously easy to over-apply. An alternative that simply increases the Ambroxan produces something that projects harder and smells cruder. More is not closer.

## The honest question about buying an alternative

Here the arithmetic differs from the other two.

Against Baccarat Rouge, a $30 alternative saves you nearly $300. Against Sauvage at $110 for a full 100ml bottle, an alternative might save you $60 or $70 — meaningful, but not transformative. And Sauvage is sold nearly everywhere, frequently discounted, and widely available in travel sizes and from decanting services.

So the case for an alternative is strongest when you want **the profile, not the bottle** — you like clean peppery amber, you do not care whose name is on it, and you would rather own three fragrances in that territory than one. It is weakest when you specifically want Sauvage, where the price gap does not justify the compromise the way it does further up the market.

That reasoning applies to a lot of the dupe market and is rarely stated: **the cheaper the original, the worse the case for a copy.**

## Adjacent originals worth knowing about

Sauvage sits in the woody-aromatic family, which is the most populated territory in men's fragrance. Several originals occupy neighbouring ground:

- **[Bleu de Chanel](/fragrance/bleu-de-chanel/)** — the other default. Woody-aromatic, drier, more incense and vetiver in the base, less amber.
- **[Allure Homme Sport](/fragrance/allure-homme-sport/)** — cleaner and more citrus-forward, a lighter proposition from the same house.
- **[Sauvage Elixir](/fragrance/sauvage-elixir/)** — Dior's own concentrated reinterpretation, woody-spicy rather than fresh; worth trying before assuming you want a copy of the original.
- **[Acqua di Gio Profumo](/fragrance/acqua-di-gio-profumo/)** — aquatic-woody and considerably more marine, for anyone who liked Sauvage's freshness but not its pepper.
- **[Viking](/fragrance/viking/)** — Creed's take on peppery woody-aromatic, at a much higher price.
