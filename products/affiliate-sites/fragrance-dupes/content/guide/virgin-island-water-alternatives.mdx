[Creed Virgin Island Water](/fragrance/virgin-island-water/) is a Citrus Tropical fragrance, and its name is close to a recipe. Our catalogue lists **coconut, lime, sugar cane, mandarin and bergamot** on top, **white rum, ylang-ylang and ginger** in the heart, and **musk, jasmine and copra** in the base.

It rates **8/10 for freshness**, **7/10 for sweetness** and **6/10 for longevity**, with very little woodiness. It is a summer fragrance in the plainest sense: bright, sweet and built around a drink.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Coconut, Lime, Sugar Cane, Mandarin, Bergamot |
| Heart | White Rum, Ylang-Ylang, Ginger |
| Base | Musk, Jasmine, Copra |

Copra is dried coconut flesh. It matters because the fragrance is not only fresh coconut: copra is the more oily, creamy side of the same idea, which is what keeps the composition from reading as a sweet-shop coconut. A copy that has fresh coconut and lime but not that creamy side will smell lighter and sweeter than the original.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance.

**Adventure by AromaPassions** is paired with Virgin Island Water by the seller, not by us. The product title on the retailer's page reads "Inspired by" Creed Virgin Island Water, and that is the seller's claim.

The declared list captures the core of the original's accord: **coconut, lime, ginger, white rum, sugar cane and ylang-ylang** are all there, along with mandarin, jasmine and musk. In terms of what makes this fragrance recognisable, the cocktail idea is present.

Two differences are worth stating:

- **White bergamot** appears where our catalogue records plain bergamot. We count that as a difference rather than rewriting the seller's word, which holds the match down slightly.
- **Copra is not declared.** The listing names hibiscus instead. If the creamy dried-coconut side matters to you, this is the gap to think about.

AromaPassions publishes one flat list of materials, not a pyramid, so the top, heart and base split on our listing is ours. We therefore do not make claims about when each material appears on skin. We can say what the list includes and what it lacks.

## Why price is the argument here, and not the only one

Virgin Island Water is sold as a luxury house fragrance, and the gap to a small-batch dupe house is large. The live comparison page shows current prices; we do not quote them in a guide, because they move.

The price gap is a reason to look, not a reason to expect the same fragrance. Coconut and rum are both easy to describe and hard to make convincing. Cheap versions tend to go in one of two directions: sunscreen-like coconut, or sweet candy. Reading the declared list for something that balances the two is more useful than reading a long list of marketing adjectives.

## Reading the two lists side by side

It helps to sort the materials into three groups rather than counting matches.

- **Shared:** coconut, lime, mandarin, ginger, white rum, sugar cane, ylang-ylang, jasmine and musk appear in both lists.
- **Worded differently:** white bergamot in the listing, plain bergamot in our catalogue. Perfumers use the word "white" in different ways, and we have not tried to settle what the seller means by it.
- **Different:** copra is in our catalogue entry and not in the listing, and hibiscus is in the listing and not in our catalogue entry.

Seen this way the overlap is high and the differences are specific, which is more useful than a single percentage. The live comparison page applies our scoring to exactly these lists, and the score it shows is capped and, for a listing whose layers we split ourselves, reduced.

## How to decide

**You want the cocktail idea: coconut, lime, rum.** Those are declared in the listing, which makes it worth comparing closely.

**You want the creamy, oily coconut finish.** Be cautious. Copra is the material the listing does not declare.

**You mostly want a warm-weather coconut fragrance and not specifically this one.** Then the question is about the style, and you have more room to choose.

## Where claims come from

The notes and facets for Virgin Island Water are our catalogue's. The note list for Adventure is the one its seller published, and the pairing is the seller's own description. We have not smelled either bottle, and the listing has not been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. [How we score is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Look for creamy materials** in the declared list if you want a rounder coconut, and for what replaces them if not.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size**, and compare like with like before judging value.
- **Treat strength and longevity claims as the seller's**, not as findings.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin before you commit to a full bottle.
