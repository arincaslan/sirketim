[Yves Saint Laurent Y](/fragrance/y/) is a Woody Aromatic fragrance built to be easy to like. Our catalogue puts **apple, bergamot and ginger** on top, **sage, juniper berries and geranium** in the heart, and **tonka bean, cedar and amberwood** in the base.

It rates **7/10 for freshness**, **7/10 for longevity** and **6/10 for sillage**, with low sweetness. In practice that describes a bright, clean, crowd-friendly opening on a dry woody finish. Anyone shopping for an alternative is shopping for that brightness without the weight, so it is the first thing to hold on to.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Apple, Bergamot, Ginger |
| Heart | Sage, Juniper Berries, Geranium |
| Base | Tonka Bean, Cedar, Amberwood |

Nine materials, and none of them is rare. Apple, ginger and bergamot are common building blocks, and amberwood is a widely used woody-amber material. That is part of why Y is copied: there is no single expensive ingredient to leave out. What is harder to reproduce is the balance. The sage and juniper give the opening a green, slightly herbal edge, and without them an apple-and-ginger fragrance reads as a generic fruity fresh scent.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance, and we would rather say that than pad the page.

**Cool by AromaPassions** is paired with Y by the seller, not by us. The product title on the retailer's page reads "Inspired by" Yves Saint Laurent Y EDP, and that is the seller's claim.

The seller declares all nine of Y's materials and adds two more: **vetiver and olibanum**. The high overlap is therefore largely a fact about how the seller wrote the list, not a finding of ours, and it should be read that way.

The two additions do point somewhere. Olibanum is frankincense: dry and resinous. Vetiver is earthy and rooty. Both pull away from the clean apple-and-ginger freshness toward something smokier and more mineral. Our verdict is that this is the same opening over a heavier, more incense-like finish.

One caution about how this listing is described. AromaPassions publishes a single flat list of materials, not a pyramid. The split into top, heart and base on our listing is ours, which is why we do not make claims here that depend on which layer a note sits in. We can say what the list includes and what it lacks. We cannot say how it unfolds on skin.

## Why "same notes" does not settle it

Two fragrances can share a note list and still wear differently, for three reasons.

- **Added materials change the centre of gravity.** Y is deliberately uncomplicated. Adding frankincense and vetiver to the same list makes a different fragrance, not a stronger copy.
- **Concentration is a separate variable.** The listing is described as an extrait de parfum, while the original is an eau de parfum. A higher concentration does not mean a closer match, and what a seller calls a concentration is the seller's description rather than something we have tested.
- **A list does not carry proportions.** Nine materials in one order of magnitude of dose and nine in another are the same list on paper.

## How to decide

**You like Y's bright start and do not mind a drier, more resinous finish.** The listed alternative is worth reading closely, because the additions are exactly what changes the finish.

**You want the clean, uncomplicated version.** Be cautious. A listing that adds incense and vetiver is more complicated than the original by design.

**You want a different route into the same mood.** The originals below are not copies, but they cover the same ground in different ways.

## Where claims come from

The notes and facets for Y are our catalogue's. The note list for Cool is the one its seller published, and the pairing is the seller's own description. We have not smelled either bottle, and the listing has not been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. [How we score is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Read the full note list.** Look for what the listing adds as well as what it matches.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size** on the product page, and compare like with like before judging value.
- **Be sceptical of marketing claims** such as strength or longevity promises. These are the seller's words, not our findings.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin before you commit to a full bottle.
