[Tom Ford Tobacco Vanille](/fragrance/tobacco-vanille/) is an Oriental Spicy fragrance, and the name is a fair summary of it. The pyramid in our catalogue opens on **tobacco leaf and spices**, puts **vanilla, cacao and tonka bean** in the heart, and finishes on **dried fruit and woods**.

It is not a subtle fragrance. In our catalogue it rates **9/10 for longevity**, **8/10 for sillage**, **8/10 for sweetness** and **9/10 for warmth**, with almost no freshness at all. Anyone shopping for an alternative is shopping for that density, so it is the first thing to hold on to.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Tobacco Leaf, Spices |
| Heart | Vanilla, Cacao, Tonka Bean |
| Base | Dried Fruit, Woods |

Two things are worth noticing. First, tobacco is on top, so it is the first thing you meet. Second, the sweetness comes from several places at once: vanilla, cacao, tonka and dried fruit all push the same way. A cheaper fragrance can copy "tobacco and vanilla" and still miss this, because it only has two of the sweet sources.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance, and we would rather say that than pad the page.

**Vanille En Tobacco by Fragrance World** is paired with Tobacco Vanille by the seller, not by us. The retailer's listing says "Inspired by Tobacco Vanilla", and that is the seller's claim. The pyramid on our listing is the one the seller published: black pepper and spicy notes on top, vanilla, tobacco and tonka beans in the heart, dry fruit and a sugary wood sap in the base.

Set that beside the original and the overlap is clear. **Vanilla and tonka bean** carry over, and so does **dried fruit** in the base. Those are the parts that make Tobacco Vanille smell like a warm, sweet room, and they are the parts this fragrance keeps.

The differences are in the top and the base. The original opens on tobacco leaf. This one opens on black pepper and folds the tobacco into the heart, so the first impression is a different note. The original also lists cacao and a mix of woods. This one has no cacao and reduces the base to a single sugary wood-sap note. Our verdict is that it reads sweeter and less complex than the original, which is not a flaw for people who mainly want the vanilla-tobacco mood, but it is a genuinely different opening.

## Why the word "tobacco" does not settle it

Two fragrances can both list tobacco and open very differently, because what sits next to the note changes how it reads. In the original, tobacco leaf is paired with spices on top. In the listed alternative, black pepper and unnamed spicy notes take the top and tobacco moves into the heart beside vanilla and tonka. So when you compare a tobacco alternative with the original, read the notes around the tobacco as well as the word itself, and check which layer it sits in.

## How to decide

**You want the sweet, warm centre.** Vanille En Tobacco keeps vanilla, tonka and dried fruit, and it is the one to read closely.

**You want the tobacco to lead.** Be cautious. The listing moves tobacco out of the top and puts black pepper there, so the first minutes will not match the original.

**You want more than one option.** For this original there is only one listing, so there is little to compare against. If the live listing does not suit you, the originals below are different routes into the same mood rather than copies.

## Where claims come from

The notes and facets for Tobacco Vanille are our catalogue's. The pyramid for Vanille En Tobacco is the one its seller published, and the pairing is the seller's own description. We have not smelled either bottle and the listing's pyramid has not been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. ## What to check before you buy

- **Read the top notes first.** Tobacco leaf and black pepper are different openings.
- **Count the sweet sources.** The original has vanilla, cacao, tonka and dried fruit. Fewer sweet sources usually means a simpler dry-down.
- **Check the concentration** on the bottle. Eau de parfum and extrait projection and staying power are not the same thing.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin before you commit to a full bottle, especially a heavily sweet one.
