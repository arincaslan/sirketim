Buying a fragrance you have never smelled is called a **blind buy**, and it is
how most fragrance gets bought now. It is also how most fragrance gets wasted —
a drawer of 80%-full bottles is the standard outcome of doing it badly.

You cannot remove the risk. You can make it much smaller.

## First: buy a decant, not a bottle

This is the whole article in one line, and everything below is for when you
have decided to skip it anyway.

A **decant** is a few millilitres of the real fragrance in a small atomiser,
sold by specialist splitters. Typically €4–12 for 5–10ml. You get several full
days of wear — enough to know — for a fraction of a bottle.

**Why it beats every other method:** every problem below comes from predicting
how something will smell. A decant replaces prediction with information.

The maths is not close. Five decants at €8 costs €40 and teaches you which one
you actually want. One blind-bought 100ml bottle at €90 teaches you one thing,
expensively, and cannot be returned once opened in most jurisdictions.

Buy decants **from splitters who name the batch and the source**. Avoid
unlabelled bottles from marketplaces — that is where counterfeits circulate,
and counterfeits are the one genuine safety risk in this category. See
<a href="/guide/are-fragrance-dupes-legal-and-safe">are dupes legal and
safe</a>.

## Reading reviews without being misled

If you are going to buy blind, reviews are your main input — and most people
read them wrong.

**Ignore the first paragraph.** Almost every review opens on the first ten
minutes, which is the part you will experience least. Skip to what people say
about hours three to six.

**Weight the complaints heavily.** "Doesn't last on me" and "too sweet" are
much more informative than praise, because praise clusters around whatever is
fashionable while complaints are specific.

**Find someone with your skin.** If a reviewer says a fragrance you both own
behaves a certain way on them, and it does the same on you, their other reviews
are worth more to you than a hundred strangers'.

**Discount extremes at both ends.** The five-star "compliment monster" reviews
and the one-star "smells like cleaning fluid" ones are both usually about
expectations rather than the fragrance.

**Check the review date against reformulation.** Fragrances get reworked —
often because a material was restricted for safety. A glowing 2013 review may
describe a composition that no longer exists.
<a href="/fragrance/fahrenheit">Fahrenheit</a> and
<a href="/fragrance/eau-sauvage">Eau Sauvage</a> are both frequently discussed
this way.

## Narrow by family before you shortlist

Blind-buying inside a family you already know you like is a very different risk
from blind-buying across one you have never worn.

Work out your family first — it takes an afternoon and permanently improves
every decision after it. See
<a href="/guide/fragrance-families-explained">fragrance families explained</a>.

Then use the note pyramid properly: **read the base first**, since that is where
you and everyone around you will spend the most time.
<a href="/guide/how-to-read-a-note-pyramid">How to read a note pyramid</a>
covers why the list can look close while the fragrances don't smell alike.

## Check the specifics sellers hope you won't

Before adding to basket:

- **Concentration.** EDT, EDP and extrait of the "same" fragrance are usually
  different formulas, not one at three strengths. See
  <a href="/guide/fragrance-concentrations-explained">what concentration
  actually changes</a>.
- **Size, and price per millilitre.** A 30ml at €29 and a 100ml at €75 are not
  the relationship they first appear to be.
- **Batch code and expiry.** Fragrance degrades. Very old stock smells sharp and
  thin.
- **Return policy on opened items.** Usually none. Assume the purchase is final.
- **Who is actually selling it** on a marketplace — the listing brand and the
  seller are often different businesses.

## When not to blind buy at all

Some purchases are simply bad bets:

- **Anything over about €100** without a decant first. The saving from getting
  it right dwarfs the cost of testing.
- **A family you have never worn.** If you have never liked an amber, a
  well-reviewed amber is still a coin flip.
- **Anything described mainly as "unique," "polarising," or "you'll either love
  it or hate it."** Those descriptions are accurate, and half the outcomes are
  bad.
- **A gift**, unless you know the person's collection. Fragrance is the most
  personal thing you can buy someone and the least likely to land blind.

## The short method

1. Work out your **family** from three fragrances you already like.
2. Shortlist within it, reading the **base notes** and the **facets**, not the
   headline.
3. Compare **price per ml** and check the **concentration**.
4. **Buy a decant.** Wear it for a full day, twice.
5. Buy the bottle only after that.
