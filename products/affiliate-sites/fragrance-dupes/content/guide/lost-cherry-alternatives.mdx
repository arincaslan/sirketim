[Tom Ford Lost Cherry](/fragrance/lost-cherry/) is a Gourmand Oriental, and "cherry" undersells how it is built. The pyramid in our catalogue opens on **black cherry, bitter almond and sour cherry**, moves to **Turkish rose, jasmine sambac and plum**, and settles on **Peru balsam, tonka bean, vetiver, sandalwood and patchouli**.

The common mistake is to treat a cherry fragrance as a sweet one. This one carries a lot of sweetness (9/10 in our catalogue), but its character comes from the sour and bitter notes cutting through it.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Black Cherry, Bitter Almond, Sour Cherry |
| Heart | Turkish Rose, Jasmine Sambac, Plum |
| Base | Peru Balsam, Tonka Bean, Vetiver, Sandalwood, Patchouli |

In our catalogue it rates **8/10 for longevity** and **7/10 for sillage**. Almond, rose and cherry together read as a liqueur-like, slightly medicinal sweetness, and the woods in the base keep it from becoming candy.

## Two listings, two different readings of the same idea

We list two alternatives, and they reached the list by different routes. One seller names Lost Cherry as its inspiration; the other pairing is our own editorial judgement. The verdict on each listing is ours.

**Naked Cherry No. 33 by The CLONE** is the closer of the two. The seller's own listing describes it as "Inspired by Lost Cherry", and that is the seller's claim, not ours. Its published materials include black cherry, bitter almond, Turkish rose, jasmine sambac, Peru balsam, tonka, sandalwood and vetiver, so the skeleton is genuinely shared. What differs is how the cherry is drawn: it adds cherry liqueur and griotte syrup, which reads boozier and more candied, and it lists cedar and clove where the original has patchouli, so it should be spicier and less earthy underneath.

**Mayar Cherry Intense by Lattafa** is the weaker pairing, and our verdict says so plainly. It is also our pairing rather than the seller's: no retailer describes it as a Lost Cherry alternative. Both are cherry gourmands with patchouli underneath. But Lost Cherry is built on a sour, almond-tinged black cherry with rose and plum, and Mayar Cherry Intense is jam, with strawberry, cacao and vanilla throughout. It delivers the idea of an expensive cherry fragrance, and not the bitter-almond edge.

## Why the almond matters

Bitter almond is the note that stops a cherry fragrance reading as syrup. Take it away and what remains is sweet fruit, which is pleasant and common. Keep it, and the fragrance has a sharper, more adult edge. When you compare any cherry alternative with Lost Cherry, check for that bitterness before you check for the cherry itself, because cherry is the easy part to reproduce and the contrast is the part that gets lost.

## How to decide

**You want the structure.** Naked Cherry No. 33 is the one to read closely. It shares most of the original's named materials and changes the style of the cherry.

**You want the mood and not the match.** Mayar Cherry Intense is a straightforwardly sweet cherry gourmand. It is a fair choice if you like sweet fruit and cacao, and a poor one if the almond is what you came for.

**You are unsure.** Look at the notes before the name. If the top of an alternative lists strawberry or jam where the original lists sour cherry and almond, you are buying a different fragrance in the same colour.

## Where claims come from

Mayar Cherry Intense's pyramid is the one its seller published. Naked Cherry No. 33's seller publishes its materials as a flat list with no top, heart or base, so the split into layers on our listing is ours, and its score carries a penalty for exactly that. We have not smelled either product, and neither pyramid has been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. [How we score is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Read the top notes.** The sour and bitter notes are what separates Lost Cherry from a generic cherry.
- **Check what the base uses.** Patchouli gives the original an earthier floor; cedar and clove give a spicier one.
- **Look at the concentration.** An extrait is a stronger formulation than an eau de parfum, and may project differently from what you expect.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin first, especially if it is heavily sweet.
