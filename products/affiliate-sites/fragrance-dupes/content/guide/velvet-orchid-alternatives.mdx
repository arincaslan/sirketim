[Tom Ford Velvet Orchid](/fragrance/velvet-orchid/) is an Oriental Floral fragrance and one of the heavier ones in our catalogue. We list **bergamot, mandarin, honey and rum** on top, **orchid, jasmine, rose and narcissus** in the heart, and **vanilla, sandalwood, myrrh and suede** in the base.

It rates **8/10 for sweetness**, **8/10 for warmth**, **8/10 for longevity** and **8/10 for sillage**, in the Beast Mode band, with very little freshness. Anyone shopping for an alternative is shopping for that density, and it is easy to underestimate how much of the character is the combination of honey, rum and suede rather than any single flower.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Bergamot, Mandarin, Honey, Rum |
| Heart | Orchid, Jasmine, Rose, Narcissus |
| Base | Vanilla, Sandalwood, Myrrh, Suede |

Two things stand out. First, honey and rum sit at the very top of the list, so the sweetness is not only floral sweetness. Second, the base mixes a sweet material (vanilla) with resinous and woody ones (myrrh, sandalwood) and suede, so the finish is not one-note sweet either. Both observations come from reading our catalogue entry, not from wearing the bottle.

This is also why the facets read the way they do. A fragrance this warm and this sweet has almost no room for freshness, and a copy that lets freshness in, or pushes sweetness even higher, changes the balance. When you compare a listing, ask which direction it moves the balance, not only whether the names match.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance.

**Glimmer by AromaPassions** is paired with Velvet Orchid by the seller, not by us. The product title on the retailer's page reads "Inspired by" Tom Ford Velvet Orchid, and that is the seller's claim.

The listing declares **nineteen** materials against the original's sixteen in our catalogue, so part of the lower overlap is breadth rather than disagreement. The core of the original is declared present: rum, honey, orchid, jasmine, rose, vanilla, suede and sandalwood, along with mandarin, bergamot, narcissus and myrrh. That is the boozy, honeyed floral the original is known for.

The additions are where it differs. The seller declares a fuller floral bouquet than Tom Ford does, with **heliotrope, hyacinth, magnolia and orange blossom**, plus **Peru balsam and labdanum**. Those balsamic materials point toward a heavier, sweeter close than the original's comparatively suede-soft one. Our verdict is that this is a denser interpretation rather than a copy.

AromaPassions publishes a single flat list, not a pyramid, so the top, heart and base division on our listing is ours. For that reason we make no claims here that depend on which layer a material sits in.

## Why more notes is not the same as closer

It is tempting to read a longer list as a better copy. For a fragrance like this it often works the other way.

- **The original is edited.** Sixteen materials, and its identity is the contrast between sweet and dark. Adding more florals and more resins blurs that contrast.
- **Balsams push sweetness further.** Peru balsam and labdanum are warm and sweet, so a list that adds them to an already sweet fragrance moves in one direction.
- **A list says nothing about dose.** Whether the honey or the heliotrope leads is not something a note list tells you.

## How to decide

**You want the honey, rum and suede mood and are happy with a richer, more balsamic version.** The listing is worth reading closely, because its additions explain how it differs.

**You want the leaner, suede-soft finish of the original.** Be cautious, since the listing adds exactly the materials that make the finish heavier.

**You want a different route into the same family.** The originals below are not copies, but they overlap in useful ways.

## Where claims come from

The notes and facets for Velvet Orchid are our catalogue's. The note list for Glimmer is the one its seller published, and the pairing is the seller's own description. We have not smelled either bottle, and the listing has not been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. [How we score is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Compare the full lists.** Look at what the listing adds, not only at what it shares.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size**, and compare like with like before judging value.
- **Treat strength and longevity claims as the seller's**, not as findings.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin, especially a heavy, sweet one.
