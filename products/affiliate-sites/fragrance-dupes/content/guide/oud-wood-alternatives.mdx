[Tom Ford Oud Wood](/fragrance/oud-wood/) is one of the reference woody fragrances in our catalogue, and it is deliberately restrained. The notes are **rosewood, cardamom and Chinese pepper** on top, **oud and sandalwood** in the heart, and **tonka bean, vanilla and amber** at the base.

The oud is surrounded, not shouted. In our catalogue it scores **9/10 for woody depth** but only **6/10 for sillage** (Moderate), with **8/10 longevity**. That quietness is a large part of why people reach for it, and also why a louder oud will not feel like a substitute.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Rosewood, Cardamom, Chinese Pepper |
| Heart | Oud, Sandalwood |
| Base | Tonka Bean, Vanilla, Amber |

## Why "oud" on the label is not the whole answer

Oud is a family of materials with very different characters, and a fragrance can be built around it in many ways. Oud Wood's way is soft: the oud sits among sandalwood, tonka and vanilla, with cardamom and pepper keeping the top lively. A fragrance that puts oud under roses, or under leather, will share a word on the label and very little else. So with this original especially, read the whole pyramid, not only the middle.

## Four listings, four routes

We list four alternatives. Three are paired by their sellers, who describe them as inspired by or reminiscent of Oud Wood, and we quote those as the sellers' claims. The fourth pairing is our own editorial judgement. The verdict on every listing is ours.

**Ultimatum No. 53 by The CLONE.** The seller's listing reads "Inspired by Tom Ford Oud Wood". Our verdict: its listed materials include all three of the original's base notes (tonka bean, amber, vanilla) and both of its heart notes, oud and sandalwood. What is missing is the opening: of rosewood, cardamom and Chinese pepper it lists only cardamom, so the bright first minutes are likely to be thinner, and its vetiver should pull it drier and earthier. It is sold as an extrait, so it may project harder than the original's quiet eau de parfum.

**Qaa'ed by Lattafa.** The seller's listing describes it as "inspired by tf oud wood". The heart is an exact note-for-note match, oud and sandalwood and nothing else. It diverges at both ends: a warmer saffron, cinnamon and cardamom top, and a base of vanilla, amber and leather in place of tonka. Expect a more oriental, leathery drydown.

**Jean Lowe Matiere by Maison Alhambra.** This is the pairing that is ours rather than a seller's, and it is not a substitute; our verdict says so. Both are built on oud, but this is a rose-oud, with blackcurrant and florals in front and patchouli and incense beneath. If you want the Tom Ford, it is the wrong bottle. If you like oud with rose over it, it is a different, fuller fragrance.

**Mohra by Lattafa.** The retailer's copy calls it "reminiscent of Tom Ford's Oud Wood", but its published notes list no oud at all. The overlap is sandalwood plus a pepper note. By its own pyramid it is a rose, pepper and cedarwood fragrance with cashmeran and labdanum. Buy it as its own thing.

## Choosing between them

**You want the closest set of materials.** Ultimatum No. 53 lists the original's base notes and its oud and sandalwood. Check the opening against your expectations.

**You want the oud and are happy with a different frame.** Qaa'ed puts the same heart in a spicier, leatherier setting.

**You like oud but not necessarily this one.** Jean Lowe Matiere is the choice, with the understanding that it is a different fragrance.

**You want a woody-spicy fragrance and the Oud Wood label is incidental.** Mohra, on its own terms.

## Where claims come from

Three of the four pyramids are the ones their sellers published. Ultimatum No. 53's seller publishes a flat list of materials with no top, heart or base, so the split into layers on our listing is ours, and its score carries a penalty for exactly that. "Inspired by" and "reminiscent of" are quoted from the sellers' listings. We have not smelled any of the products, and none of the pyramids has been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. The [methodology is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Does the pyramid list oud?** Not every fragrance sold alongside oud ones does.
- **Compare the opening.** Rosewood and Chinese pepper are what give the original its first impression.
- **Check the concentration.** A stronger concentration may be louder than the original.
- **Patch-test** any new fragrance on a small area of skin first.
