[Jo Malone London Wood Sage & Sea Salt](/fragrance/wood-sage-sea-salt/) is an Aromatic fragrance built around restraint. Our catalogue puts **sea salt and ambrette seed** on top, **sage** in the heart, and **red algae and driftwood** in the base.

It rates **6/10 for freshness**, **4/10 for longevity** and **2/10 for sillage**, with very low sweetness. Our catalogue lists its longevity at roughly two to four hours and its sillage as intimate. Jo Malone sells it as a Cologne rather than an eau de toilette or eau de parfum, and that label matters: this is meant to sit close and fade early. Anyone shopping for an alternative should decide first whether they want that gentleness, or want the same mood with more staying power.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Sea Salt, Ambrette Seed |
| Heart | Sage |
| Base | Red Algae, Driftwood |

Five materials. That is a very short list, and it is why the scent is hard to describe and easy to misjudge. Sea salt and red algae are accords rather than single ingredients in the usual sense: they are perfumery shorthand for a briny, mineral, slightly sulphurous impression. Ambrette seed is a musky, faintly pear-like material. Sage supplies the dry green edge, and driftwood the pale, weathered wood.

Because there is so little in it, a single swapped material is a large share of the whole. That cuts both ways when you compare a copy against it.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance, and we would rather say so than pad the page.

**Nature by AromaPassions** is paired with Wood Sage & Sea Salt by the seller, not by us. The product title on the retailer's page reads "Inspired by" Jo Malone London Wood Sage & Sea Salt, and that is the seller's claim.

The seller declares **sea salt, sage, grapefruit, ambrette (musk mallow) and seaweed**. Two points follow.

- **The two materials the original is named for, sea salt and sage, are both declared.** That is the important part of any comparison.
- **The rest is a substitution, partly of vocabulary.** The original's red algae becomes seaweed, which is adjacent rather than identical. Ambrette seed becomes "ambrette (musk mallow)", which is the same plant under a different designation. Driftwood is not declared at all, and grapefruit appears where the original has no citrus.

Our verdict is that the declaration is too thin to tell you much either way. A good part of the gap on paper comes from wording, not composition, and we do not rewrite a seller's wording into ours.

One caution about how this listing is described. AromaPassions publishes a single flat list of materials, not a pyramid. The split into top, heart and base on our listing is ours, which is why we make no claims here that depend on which layer a note sits in. We can say what the list includes and what it lacks. We cannot say how it unfolds on skin.

## Why a short note list is hard to compare

- **Proportions do all the work.** With five materials, how much salt, how much sage and how much musk decide whether the result smells like a beach or like a cleaning product. A list does not carry proportions.
- **Accords are not ingredients.** "Sea salt" and "seaweed" name an impression, and two houses can build it from entirely different materials.
- **Quiet is a design choice.** The original is intentionally short-lived. The listing is described by its seller as an extrait de parfum, and a higher concentration will usually mean more presence, which is not the same as a closer match. What a seller calls a concentration is the seller's description rather than something we have tested.

## How to decide

**You love the original's salty, mineral character and wish it lasted.** The listed alternative declares both defining materials and is described at a higher concentration, so it is worth reading closely. Expect more presence than the original, and expect it to differ in the details.

**You want the original's hush as well.** Be cautious. Part of what you are paying for is the restraint, and a stronger version is a different experience.

**You want a different route to the same mood.** The originals below are not copies, but they cover nearby ground.

## Where claims come from

The notes and facets for Wood Sage & Sea Salt are our catalogue's. The note list for Nature is the one its seller published, and the pairing is the seller's own description. We have not smelled either bottle, and the listing has not been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. [How we score is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Read the full note list.** Look for what the listing swaps and omits, driftwood in particular.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size** on the product page, and compare like with like before judging value.
- **Be sceptical of marketing claims** about strength or longevity. These are the seller's words, not our findings.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin before you commit to a full bottle.
