[Dior Sauvage Elixir](/fragrance/sauvage-elixir/) is a Woody Spicy fragrance sold in the Elixir concentration. It is a different animal from the fresh, peppery [Sauvage](/fragrance/sauvage/) that shares its name, and shoppers who expect one to behave like the other are often surprised.

Our catalogue puts **cinnamon, nutmeg, cardamom and grapefruit** on top, **lavender and licorice** in the heart, and **sandalwood, amber, patchouli and vetiver** in the base. It rates **8/10 for warmth**, **8/10 for woodiness** and only **4/10 for sweetness**. Longevity is listed at roughly nine to twelve hours, with sillage in the Beast Mode band.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Cinnamon, Nutmeg, Cardamom, Grapefruit |
| Heart | Lavender, Licorice |
| Base | Sandalwood, Amber, Patchouli, Vetiver |

Three things shape the character. The baking spices make the opening warm rather than fresh. The grapefruit keeps that warmth from turning heavy. And the licorice gives the middle an unusual dark-sweet, anise-like edge that most spicy woody scents do not have.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance, and we would rather say so than pad the page.

**Embassy Royal Extrait by Afnan** is paired with Sauvage Elixir by us. No retailer describes it as an alternative to the Dior, so the pairing is our own editorial judgement, based on the seller's published note list.

The seller declares **nutmeg, cinnamon, cardamom and grapefruit** on top, **lavender** in the heart, and **sandalwood, amber, patchouli and vetiver** in the base.

- **Every note except one overlaps.** The top and the base match the original's, material for material.
- **The licorice is missing.** It is the one material in our catalogue's heart that the declaration does not include.
- **The heart is thinner on paper.** One declared material against the original's two.

Our verdict is that this is the closest match we have published for any fragrance in the catalogue, and also that the gap is the interesting part. Without licorice it reads as a more straightforward spicy-woody and a little less strange. The pyramid is the one its seller published, we have not smelled the bottle, and the listing has not been independently verified.

## Why one missing note matters

A note list that matches on seven of eight materials sounds like a near-identical scent. It may be, but a list says nothing about **quantities**. Two fragrances can share every material and still smell different if one doses the spices heavily and the other lightly. The licorice is also the sort of material that is easy to leave off a list and hard to replace, because nothing else in the declaration does the same job.

So treat the overlap as a good reason to look, not as proof of a match. This is also why [our published scores](/about/#methodology) are capped for listings we have not checked ourselves.

## What the opening tells you, and what it does not

The first ten minutes of a spice-led fragrance are the easiest part to reproduce, because cinnamon, nutmeg and cardamom are loud and familiar. Most of the comparison work happens later. The licorice and lavender sit in the middle of the structure, and the sandalwood, amber, patchouli and vetiver decide what is left on your clothes at the end of the day. If you are testing an alternative, spray it on a card and on skin, then come back to both after a few hours rather than judging at the first sniff.

If you already own the original, hold the two side by side and ask a single question: does the middle feel strange in the same way? That is where the difference between the two is most likely to show.

## Concentration is not a ranking

The original is sold as an Elixir and the alternative as an Extrait de Parfum. Those are labels each brand applies to its own bottle. We would not read either as a promise about strength or staying power, and neither should a buyer comparing the two.

## How to decide

**You want the exact spiced-and-licorice character.** The listing is missing the licorice, and we would not claim otherwise.

**You mostly want the warm spice-and-wood profile.** The overlap is strong and the listing is worth reading in full.

**You want the fresher, peppery Sauvage instead.** This is a different fragrance, and the Elixir and its alternative are not the place to look.

## What to check before you buy

- **Read the whole note list on the product page** and look for the licorice or anything sweet-dark in the middle.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size** the seller states.
- **Treat strength and longevity claims as the seller's words**, not our findings.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin before you commit to a full bottle.

## Neighbouring originals worth knowing

- **[Sauvage](/fragrance/sauvage/)** by Dior is the fresh side: bergamot and pepper over ambroxan and cedar.
- **[Dior Homme Intense](/fragrance/dior-homme-intense/)** by Dior is lavender, iris and pear over vetiver and cedar, a floral-woody rather than spiced take on the house.
