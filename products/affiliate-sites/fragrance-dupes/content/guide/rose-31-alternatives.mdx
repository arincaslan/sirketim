[Le Labo Rose 31](/fragrance/rose-31/) is a Woody Floral eau de parfum that people reach for when they say they dislike rose scents. That is the point of it: the flower is there, but it is dry, spiced and a little savoury.

Our catalogue puts **coriander and cumin** on top, **rose and cedar** in the heart, and **amber, musk, vetiver and olibanum** in the base. It rates **7/10 for woodiness**, **5/10 for warmth** and only **2/10 for sweetness**. Longevity is listed at roughly six to eight hours with moderate sillage.

## What is actually in it

| Layer | Notes |
|---|---|
| Top | Coriander, Cumin |
| Heart | Rose, Cedar |
| Base | Amber, Musk, Vetiver, Olibanum |

Cumin is the unusual one. It is sharp, earthy and faintly sweaty, and it is what stops the rose reading as pretty. Cedar and vetiver keep the middle dry, and olibanum (frankincense) adds a resinous, slightly smoky tail.

Anyone looking for an alternative should first decide what they want. A rose scent with the same dry, spiced attitude is one request. Any rose at a lower cost is another, and the two lead to very different bottles.

## The one listed alternative

We list one alternative for this fragrance, and we would rather say so than pad the page.

**Velvet Rose by Lattafa** is paired with Rose 31 by us. No retailer describes it as an alternative to the Le Labo, so this is our own editorial judgement.

The seller declares **rose and patchouli** on top, **labdanum and musk** in the heart, and **musk and amber** in the base.

- **It shares the rose, the musk and the amber.** That is three overlapping materials out of the original's eight.
- **It has none of the spice or the wood.** Cumin, coriander, cedar, vetiver and olibanum are all absent from the declaration.
- **It adds patchouli and labdanum.** Both are sweeter, darker and more resinous than anything in the original's top.

Our verdict is that this is a loose comparison and the score says so. Both are rose fragrances worn by anyone, but the original surrounds its rose until the flower is almost incidental, while this one is a sweeter rose on musk and amber. Same flower, opposite intention. Its pyramid is the one its seller published, and we have not smelled it.

## Why people buy it despite disliking roses

Rose has a reputation for being powdery, sweet or old-fashioned, and that is a matter of what surrounds it. Put the same flower beside cumin and vetiver and it reads as a spice-market scent rather than a bouquet. If that is what drew you to Rose 31, the shopping question is not "which rose" but "which surroundings", and a listing that keeps the rose while changing the surroundings answers a different question from the one you are asking.

## Why this one is hard to copy

- **The character is in the supporting cast.** Remove cumin and cedar and the rose becomes an ordinary one.
- **A rose note covers many styles.** Jammy, powdery, green and peppery roses all appear as "rose" on a list.
- **Amber and musk are shared with a huge number of perfumes.** Matching on them says little.

## How to decide

**You want the exact dry, spiced rose.** The listed alternative will not give you that, and we would not claim otherwise.

**You like rose with warmth and sweetness.** The listing is worth reading, as a different style of rose rather than a copy.

**You want a spiced, woody take on the same mood.** Look at the originals below.

## Where claims come from

The notes and facets for Rose 31 are our catalogue's. The note list for Velvet Rose is the one its seller published, and the pairing is ours. The listing has not been independently verified, which is why our published scores are capped. [How we score is on our standards page](/about/#methodology).

## What to check before you buy

- **Read the full note list.** Look for spice and wood, and for sweet additions.
- **Check the concentration and bottle size** on the product page.
- **Be sceptical of strength and longevity claims.** These are the seller's words, not our findings.
- **Patch-test** a new fragrance on a small area of skin before you commit to a full bottle.
