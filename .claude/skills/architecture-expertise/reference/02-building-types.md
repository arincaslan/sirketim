# Building types: what works, and why

Figures here are **rules of thumb** from professional practice unless they cite `09-regulation-quick-reference.md`. Use them to size a first option and to sanity-check a plan. They are not limits.

## 1. Housing: the main types

| Type (TR) | Description | Where it fits | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Müstakil ev / villa | Detached, one family | Low-density zones, ayrık nizam, low KAKS | Garden relationship, privacy from neighbours, garage access, roof form |
| İkiz villa | Semi-detached pair sharing one wall | Parcels too narrow for two detached houses | The party wall carries the acoustic and fire separation; mirror the plans |
| Sıra ev | Row or terraced houses | Bitişik nizam, long frontage | Narrow fronts (5–7 m): daylight reaches only front and back, so plans are long and linear |
| Apartman, point block (nokta blok) | One core serving 2–4 units per floor | Most Turkish urban parcels (ayrık, 4–8 floors) | Every unit should touch at least two façades; the core position decides that |
| Lama blok (slab) | A long block with several cores, or a corridor | Large parcels and estates (site) | Long corridors make single-aspect units; north-facing single-aspect units are hard to sell |
| Galeri (open-corridor) access | External walkway serves the units | Mild climates, social housing | Privacy and noise for windows on the walkway |
| Kule (tower) | One core, 4–8 units per floor, high-rise | High KAKS, city centres | Fire stairs and lobby rules change above 21.50 m and 30.50 m (`09`). Wind, structure and cost rise steeply. |
| Karma (mixed-use) | Shops at ground floor, flats above | Ticaret+konut zones, main roads | Separate entrances, a taller ground floor, fire separation between uses, soft-storey risk (see `06`) |

## 2. Flat (daire) types and typical sizes

Turkish naming counts **bedrooms + living room**:
- **1+0** = stüdyo
- **1+1** = 1 bedroom + salon
- **2+1** = 2 bedrooms + salon
- **3+1** = 3 bedrooms + salon
- **4+1** = 4 bedrooms + salon

Typical **net** ranges (rule of thumb; check the local market before committing a unit mix):

| Type | Net m² (typical) | Usual composition |
|---|---|---|
| 1+0 | 30–45 | Open living and sleeping, kitchenette, bathroom |
| 1+1 | 45–65 | Antre, salon (often with open kitchen), bedroom, bathroom, balcony |
| 2+1 | 75–100 | Antre, salon, kitchen (open or closed), 2 bedrooms, bathroom (+ WC in larger ones), balcony |
| 3+1 | 105–140 | As 2+1, plus a master bedroom with ebeveyn banyosu, a guest WC and storage |
| 4+1 | 140–190 | As 3+1, plus a family room or study, more storage |

- **Gross, net and saleable area.**
  - Net (net alan) is measured inside the walls.
  - Gross (brüt) adds the walls and shafts. The flat's share of common areas (stairs, lobby, sometimes the basement) gives the saleable or contract area many developers quote.
  - Always say which one a number is.
- **Plan legality.** Every room must meet the regulation minimum (PAİY Madde 29, see `09`). Minimum is not comfort: a 9 m² bedroom with a double bed leaves about 60 cm on each side.
- **Duplex variants.** Each one comes with plan-note conditions; check the ledger.
  - **dubleks:** two levels connected by a private stair
  - **bahçe dubleksi:** ground and basement, opening to the garden
  - **çatı dubleksi:** top floor plus the roof piyes
  - **tripleks:** three levels

## 3. Apartment plan geometry (rules of thumb)

- **Unit depth.**
  - Dual-aspect flats work at about 10–14 m deep.
  - Single-aspect flats need to stay within about 7–9 m of the window wall, which means wide frontages, about 8–10 m for a 2+1.
- **Core.**
  - A stair, lift and shafts core for a 4–8 storey block takes about 20–30 m² per floor.
  - Its size comes from the stair arithmetic and the lift (`06`), not from a guess.
- **Units per core.**
  - 2 per floor gives the best flats: every unit dual-aspect, minimal corridor.
  - 3–4 per floor is efficient but creates single-aspect units. Check which way those face.
- **Efficiency.** Net usable ÷ gross floor is about 0.75–0.85 for a good point block. Below 0.75, look at corridor length and core size.
- **Compactness.** A deep, compact footprint has less façade per m², so it costs less and loses less heat. Too deep and the middle goes dark. A 10–14 m depth between two façades is a common balance for housing.
- **Stacking.** Repeat the typical floor, and stack wet rooms and kitchens vertically. The exceptions are the ground floor (entrance, lobby, possibly commercial), the top floor (roof duplex, terraces) and the basement (parking, storage, plant). Design those floors explicitly; don't copy the typical floor.

## 4. Villas and single-family houses

- **Organization.**
  - Living, kitchen and guest WC on the garden level; bedrooms upstairs, or on one level for accessibility.
  - The kitchen connects to the garden or terrace and to a service or garage entrance.
- **The entrance sequence matters more than in flats:** forecourt, door, antre, view to the garden.
- **Section.** Double-height living spaces, roof terraces and a basement (hobby room, technical room, storage) are common.
  - Basement daylight and emsal treatment follow the plan notes.
  - Pools and outbuildings follow PAİY and local rules; check the ledger.
- **Orientation.** Put the living room and main terrace on the south or garden side. Put the garage and service side toward the street or north.

## 5. Mixed-use ground floors

- **Ground floor height.** Commercial ground floors need more height than residential ones. Local plan notes and PAİY Madde 28 set the maximum; see `09`.
- **Entrances.** A separate residential entrance and lobby, never through a shop. Service access and refuse for shops.
- **Fire.** Separation between commercial and residential uses (fire-rated floor and walls, protected stair).
- **Earthquake.** A tall, open shop floor under walled flats is the classic soft or weak storey (TBDY B1/B2). Coordinate walls and columns with the engineer from the start.
- **Parking.** Commercial uses carry their own Otopark Ek-1 rates per m². Check the ledger.

## 6. Other types

| Type | Planning rules of thumb | Regulation pointer |
|---|---|---|
| Office | Daylight depth about 6–7.5 m from each façade, so a central-core plate is about 15–18 m deep. Planning grid 1.20, 1.35 or 1.50 m. Floor-to-floor about 3.60–4.20 m for services. About 8–12 m² gross per workstation including circulation. | PAİY + local plan notes, Otopark Ek-1 office rate, Yangın Yönetmeliği office occupancy |
| Hotel / pansiyon | Room module about 3.6–4.2 m wide, about 8–9 m deep including bathroom. Corridor about 1.8–2.0 m. | Turizm tesisleri belgelendirme yönetmeliği (minimum room sizes by category), Yangın Yönetmeliği accommodation rules |
| Retail (dükkan) | Frontage drives value. Back-of-house storage, WC, a service entrance. | Plan notes on ground-floor use, Otopark Ek-1 |
| School, kindergarten, health, worship | Specialized program and regulation. Treat as a new research task, not a variant of housing. | MEB, Sağlık Bakanlığı and Diyanet standards; Yangın Yönetmeliği; Sığınak thresholds (`09`) |

## 7. Choosing a type for a parcel: a first-pass method

1. From the ledger, work out the maximum footprint (TAKS × area), maximum gross (KAKS × area, plus allowed exclusions) and the floor count.
2. Set the floors from footprint × floors ≈ emsal budget. If the footprint is capped by setbacks rather than TAKS, the setback envelope decides the floor plate.
3. Divide the plate by unit size and units per core. Check that parking capacity matches the unit count (1 stall per unit baseline, `09`).
4. Pick the type and core position that gives every unit at least two façades, if the plate allows it.
5. Write it down as option A, then make B and C by changing one major decision each (see `design-reasoning`).
